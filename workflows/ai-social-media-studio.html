One image, every social format

Every platform has its own idea of the perfect image size — Instagram wants a square or a tall 4:5, X (Twitter) wants a landscape, LinkedIn wants something between, and Stories want 9:16. Manually cropping the same image for each is tedious and error-prone. AI Social Media Studio takes one source image and generates all the sizes you need in a single pass.

The payoff is both speed and consistency. You design once, then ship a matched set of visuals across every channel, so your brand looks deliberate and cohesive no matter where a follower encounters it.

Who uses it

  • Social media managers — prepare a full cross-platform set from one hero image.
  • Brands and small businesses — keep posts consistent across Instagram, X, Facebook, and LinkedIn.
  • Creators — repurpose one visual for feed, story, and post without redoing it.
  • Agencies — batch out client assets in every required format.

How to use it

  1. Upload your hero image.
  2. Select the platforms or sizes you need.
  3. The tool crops and resizes each automatically.
  4. Download the full set and post.

Design for the crop, not against it

The biggest frustration in multi-platform posting is losing important content to a crop. Design your source image with the safe zone in mind — keep the key subject and any text toward the center, and accept that the outer edges will be trimmed for narrower formats. A little planning up front saves re-doing every format later.

Keep the message consistent

Across platforms, the visual should feel like one campaign even as the aspect ratio changes. Use the same palette, the same hero subject, and the same headline hierarchy. Consistency across channels is what makes a brand look established rather than scattered.

Text size matters at feed scale

Social feeds shrink images dramatically, so any text you include needs to be large enough to read at thumbnail size. A headline that looks fine full-screen often becomes illegible in a feed. Test at small size before you commit.

Consistency across channels is a brand signal

When a follower sees your post on Instagram, then again on X, and later on LinkedIn, they should feel like they are looking at the same voice. Mismatched crops, different color treatments, and inconsistent framing quietly signal amateur, while a matched set of visuals signals deliberate and established. Batching all formats from one source image is the easiest way to guarantee that consistency.

There is also a practical saving: instead of exporting and re-exporting for each platform, you design once and ship everywhere. The hours add up quickly for anyone who posts regularly, and the fewer manual steps in your publishing routine, the fewer chances for a wrong-sized or badly-cropped image to slip through.

What this tool does not do

This tool crops, resizes, and formats an image you provide. It does not generate artwork or edit the image content itself. Cropping to different aspect ratios will trim edges, so start with a source image that has safe margins. All processing runs locally in your browser.

The hidden cost of resizing by hand

Cropping one image into six formats sounds trivial until you do it weekly. Each platform wants a different ratio, and each manual crop is a chance to lose the subject, cut off text, or produce a blurry upscale. Multiply that across a month of posts and the "quick resize" becomes a recurring time sink — and a source of subtle, embarrassing inconsistencies in your feed.

Batching the resize into a single pass removes both problems at once: the time cost and the inconsistency. You design one hero image, let the workflow produce every format, and the whole campaign ships looking deliberate and uniform. That is the difference between a feed that looks managed and one that looks improvised.

A secondary benefit is reuse. Once you have the multi-format set, you can recycle the same hero visual across a product launch, an announcement, and a follow-up post, each cropped correctly for its channel — stretching one design asset much further.
