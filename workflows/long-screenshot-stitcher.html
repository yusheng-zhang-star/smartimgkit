Stitch several screenshots into one tall image

A phone or laptop screen captures only what fits in view, but what you usually want to share is the whole thing — a full conversation thread, an entire article, a long spreadsheet. Long Screenshot Stitcher takes multiple consecutive screenshots and aligns them into a single tall image, removing the duplicated overlap automatically. No manual Photoshop alignment, and none of the ghosting or misalignment you get from shaky scroll-capture tools.

The use cases are everywhere: share a complete chat log with a friend, assemble a full web page for archival, or build a single operation-flow image from consecutive app screens. The tool detects the repeated area between neighboring captures and blends them seamlessly, which is more accurate than lining things up by hand.

Who uses it

  • Everyone — stitch chat logs and long articles to share or archive.
  • Support and customer-success teams — compile conversation threads and user-feedback screenshots for reports.
  • Bloggers and creators — turn a long article into a single tall image for social or newsletter layouts.
  • Teams writing docs — assemble step-by-step screenshots into a clean SOP.

How to use it

  1. Upload your screenshots in top-to-bottom order.
  2. The tool finds the overlap between adjacent captures.
  3. It blends them and removes the duplicated region.
  4. Download the finished tall image.

Overlap is the secret to a clean stitch

For the alignment to work, each neighboring pair of screenshots should share roughly a quarter to a third of their content. If two captures have no overlap — if you scrolled too far and skipped a chunk — the result will have a visible gap. The more consistent your scroll distance and the more stable the page content, the cleaner the stitch.

Keep your captures consistent

Do not change zoom, theme, or scroll direction between captures. Mixed orientations and inconsistent scaling are the most common causes of a wobbly, misaligned result. Clear away popups, notifications, and timestamp overlays before you start — whatever is on the screen gets stitched into the final image too.

For very long threads, split into sections

If your stitched image ends up several screen-heights tall, chat apps will aggressively compress it on preview, making the text hard to read. For very long content, break it into two or three shorter sections, or export at a controlled size so it stays legible when shared.

What this tool does not do

This is a vertical, order-based stitcher. Stitch quality depends on how consistent and overlapping your captures are; if two captures differ too much (a page changed mid-scroll, or you scrolled unevenly), the auto-alignment may drift and you will need to recapture. All processing runs locally in your browser.

Why a stitched image beats a scrolling video

When you need to show someone a long thread or page, you have two options: record a scrolling video, or stitch a single tall image. The image is usually the better choice — it can be skimmed at the reader's own pace, it is easy to archive and search, and it survives compression in a chat app far better than a shaky screen recording. A clean long image is the most shareable way to hand over a lot of vertical content.

That is why the preparation matters. Consistent captures with a little overlap produce a seamless result; sloppy captures produce a wobbly, gappy image that undermines the whole point. A few extra seconds spent keeping your screenshots even pays off in a much cleaner final image.
