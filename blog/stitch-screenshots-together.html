How to Stitch Screenshots Together into One Long Image (2026)

Published: 2026-10-18 · 9 min read

Phone makers solved long screenshots years ago and then left three holes: scrolling capture breaks on chats and apps that lazy-load, it can't merge separate screenshots taken at different times, and it doesn't exist on desktop. Stitching after the fact fills all three, and doing it in the browser turns it into a 10-second job with no upload of whatever private conversation you're preserving. This guide covers the manual workflow, the formats a stitched long image fits, and where the phone built-in is still the better tool.

If you've ever tried to share a long chat conversation, a multi-step tutorial, a full web article, or a step-by-step app walkthrough, you know the pain: the content is too long to fit in one screenshot, so you take 3 or 4 separate images, and send them one after another. The recipient has to scroll back and forth, mentally piece the images together, and half the context gets lost. It's clunky, and it looks unprofessional.

The fix is simple: stitch all those screenshots into one long, continuous image. It takes 10 seconds in our free browser-based tool, no Photoshop, no app download, and your screenshots never leave your device — which matters a lot when you're stitching together chat logs with private customer information.

Why built-in scrolling screenshots aren't enough

Both iPhones and Android phones added built-in "scrolling screenshot" features years ago, and they work great for single web pages inside Safari or Chrome. But they have three big limitations that make a dedicated stitcher necessary for most real use cases:

  • iPhone's full-page capture only works in Safari. It exports as a PDF, not an image, and you can't use it to stitch screenshots from other apps — like Slack threads, Instagram DMs, or your product management tool.
  • Android's scrolling capture cuts off at app boundaries. It can capture a long scroll inside one app, but if you want to combine screenshots from different sources — say, one from your desktop browser, one from your phone, one from a design mockup — the built-in feature can't help.
  • They can't handle repeated content. When you take overlapping screenshots while scrolling, built-in tools don't automatically cut the duplicated navigation bars, headers, or buttons between them. You end up with duplicated UI elements halfway down your long image.

When do you actually need to stitch screenshots?

Long, stitched screenshots are one of the most versatile content formats for online work:

  • Tutorials and documentation. If you're writing a how-to guide, one tall screenshot showing every step beats 10 separate images that force the reader to flip between them. Developers and technical writers use this constantly for product docs.
  • Customer support threads. If you need to show a bug report or a conversation with a customer, a single long screenshot of the entire chat is way clearer than 6 separate images out of order.
  • Social media content. Long screenshots of tweets, reviews, or reddit threads perform really well on LinkedIn, X, and Instagram carousels — people can read the whole conversation in one glance.
  • Web page captures. If you need to save a full blog post or landing page for your records, stitching 3-4 page screenshots is faster than trying to use a full-page capture extension that misses half the page.

Step-by-step: stitch screenshots in 4 steps

  1. Take your screenshots in order. Scroll slowly as you capture each one, so the bottom of one screenshot overlaps the top of the next by about 50 pixels. That overlap makes alignment easier and prevents missing content between frames. Make sure all screenshots have roughly the same width — if one is a desktop screenshot at 1920px and another is a phone screenshot at 400px wide, they'll look misaligned when you stitch them.
  2. Upload them to the tool in order. Drag and drop all your screenshots into the upload area. The tool processes them locally in your browser, so there's no upload progress bar, and your sensitive screenshots (chat logs, customer data, internal app screens) never leave your computer.
  3. Choose vertical (top-to-bottom) or horizontal (left-to-right) layout. 95% of the time you'll want vertical stitching for long screenshots. Horizontal is useful when you're putting two comparison screenshots side by side, like before/after edits.
  4. Adjust the gap and export. Set the gap between screenshots to 0 for a seamless look, or add a small 10-20px white padding if you want a little breathing room. Export as PNG for crisp text, and you're done.

Common stitching mistakes (and how to fix them)

  • Duplicated content between frames. If your screenshots overlap too much, you'll end up with the same navigation bar or paragraph appearing twice halfway down the long image. The fix: crop the overlapping part of the second screenshot before stitching, so the duplicated UI element is gone.
  • Misaligned edges. If you took screenshots while scrolling slowly and shifted the window a little, the edges won't line up. The tool auto-aligns the center of each screenshot, but if they're drastically different widths, you can add a white background border to make the whole long image a uniform width.
  • Text looks blurry after stitching. That happens when you export as JPG. Screenshots are full of sharp text and UI edges — save them as PNG to preserve that sharpness. JPG works fine for photo collages, but it smudges text.
  • Long image gets compressed when you share it. If you post a 20,000-pixel-tall long screenshot to Discord or Slack, it will auto-compress and text will become unreadable. Split it into 2-3 long images instead of one super-long one, to keep text readable.

A lot of the online "free screenshot stitcher" tools you find on Google work by uploading all your screenshots to their server, stitching them there, then letting you download the result. That's a terrible idea for sensitive screenshots: if you're stitching a customer support chat, internal dashboard, or personal conversation, that private content is sent to a random third-party server, where it could be stored or accessed.

Our tool does 100% of the stitching locally in your browser. The images never leave your device — the tool draws them to an offscreen canvas, stitches them together, and exports the result directly to your downloads folder. No account, no watermark, no upload, no privacy risk.

When stitching runs locally, your sensitive financial documents never leave your laptop, and you can export a single long PDF or PNG directly for your application, no privacy compromise required. FAQ

How do I combine multiple screenshots into one image? Use a browser-based stitcher: add the screenshots in order, set overlap or spacing, and download one long PNG. Local tools do this without uploading the screenshots anywhere.

How do I handle overlapping areas like status bars? Either crop the duplicated status bar from each screenshot before stitching, or use a tool with automatic overlap detection that aligns the scrolled content.

What width should stitched chat screenshots use? Keep the original width of every screenshot; mixing widths forces scaling that blurs text. For phone screenshots that is usually 1080 or 1170px.

PNG or JPEG for the stitched result? PNG for text-heavy screenshots: it is lossless, so small text stays crisp. JPEG artifacts blur characters, and screenshots still compress well as PNG.
