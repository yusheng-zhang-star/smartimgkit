How to Find Where a Photo Was Taken (And Remove GPS Data to Protect Your Privacy)

📅 September 10, 2026 · ⏱️ 8 min read · 👤 SmartImgKit Team

Every time you take a photo with your smartphone, it secretly records exactly where you were — down to a few meters. That sunset photo from your vacation? It has your GPS coordinates embedded. The picture of your new home? It reveals your address.

In this guide, we'll show you how to find where a photo was taken using EXIF GPS data, how to visualize your photos on an interactive map, and — crucially — how to remove that location data before sharing photos online.

⚠️ Why this matters

Stalkers, burglars, and identity thieves can use photo GPS data to find your home, track your routine, and know when you're away. A 2023 study found that over 70% of social media photos still contain removable location data.

What is EXIF GPS Data?

EXIF (Exchangeable Image File Format) is metadata embedded in image files by cameras and smartphones. It includes:

  • GPS coordinates — latitude and longitude of where the photo was taken
  • Camera info — make, model, lens, serial number
  • Settings — aperture, shutter speed, ISO, focal length
  • Date & time — exact timestamp of the shot
  • Software — apps used to edit or export the image

When location services are enabled (which is the default on both iPhone and Android), your phone records GPS coordinates in every photo you take.

When You Should Remove EXIF Data

🚫 Always remove EXIF before sharing:
  • Photos of your home or apartment
  • Pictures of your kids at school or home
  • Real estate listings (don't reveal your address before sale)
  • Workplace photos (could expose sensitive locations)
  • Any photo shared publicly on social media
  • eBay/Facebook Marketplace listings

When GPS Data is Actually Useful

It's not all about privacy — GPS data in photos can be incredibly useful:

  • Travel memory mapping — see your vacation route on a map
  • Real estate — verify property locations
  • Photography organization — sort photos by location
  • Field research — scientists and researchers geotag specimens
  • Fitness & outdoor — GPX export for Strava and Garmin

The key is control: know when GPS data is there, and choose when to remove it.

How to Disable GPS on Your Phone

iPhone

  1. Go to Settings → Privacy & Security → Location Services
  2. Scroll to Camera
  3. Select Never to disable geotagging

Android

  1. Open Camera app → Settings
  2. Toggle off Location tags or Save location
💡 Our recommendation

Keep GPS enabled while traveling (for photo mapping), but always run photos through a batch EXIF remover before posting online. You get the best of both worlds.
