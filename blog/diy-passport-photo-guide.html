DIY Passport Photo Guide: Take It At Home, Save $15

Published: August 22, 2026 · 7 min read

Last year I waited 6 weeks for my US passport renewal because the CVS photo booth took a picture where my head was 2mm too low, and the State Department rejected it. I ended up re-taking the photo at my kitchen table, uploading it to our free passport photo tool, and it got approved on the first try. You don't need to pay $15 at a pharmacy, and you don't need to go to a photo studio. Here's exactly how to do it right.

Official Requirements You Have to Hit

Different countries have slightly different specs, but the rules are almost identical:

  • Background: Pure white, no texture, no shadows. No wall outlets, no furniture visible behind you.
  • Lighting: Even front lighting, no shadows under your chin or on the side of your face. No flash red-eye.
  • Expression: Neutral, closed mouth, eyes open looking directly at the camera. No smiling, no frowns.
  • Clothing: Everyday clothes, no uniform, no hats (religious headwear is allowed), no glasses (even prescription glasses need to be removed — this trips up so many people).
  • Head size: Your face must take up 50-69% of the photo height. Too small = rejected, too big = rejected.
Most people get this wrong when they take their own photo: they stand too far from the camera, so their head is too small, or they stand sideways to a window, so there's a shadow on half their face.

Step-by-Step: Take the Perfect Photo At Home

  1. Set up your background: Stand in front of a plain white wall. If your wall is off-white, tape a white bed sheet to it — that works perfectly.
  2. Lighting setup: Stand facing a large window (not direct sun). The soft natural light from the window will evenly light your face. If it's dark outside, use two desk lamps on either side of you, both at face level, no overhead light.
  3. Take the photo: Use your phone's rear camera, hold it at eye level, 4-6 feet away. Take 5-10 shots so you can pick the best one. Don't use selfie mode — front camera distorts your face.
  4. Upload to our tool: Our passport photo maker automatically detects your face, crops it to the exact 2×2 size, cuts the background to pure white, and checks that your head is the right size. No Photoshop needed, runs locally in your browser.
  5. Print or upload: For paper applications, print on 4×6 inch photo paper at your local drugstore — it costs 35 cents, and you get 6 passport photos on one sheet. For online applications, download the digital file and upload it directly to the government website.

Common Rejection Reasons (Don't Make These Mistakes)

  • Glare on glasses: Even if you wear glasses every day, take them off for the photo. Any reflection on the lenses is an automatic rejection.
  • Shadows on the wall: If your shadow is visible on the wall behind you, move further away from the wall. Stand 3 feet in front of the wall, not 6 inches.
  • Smiling: Even a slight closed-mouth smile can get rejected. Think about something boring, keep your face completely neutral.
  • Selfie angle: Taking the photo from below or above makes your head shape wrong. Hold the camera exactly at eye level.
  • Filters: No Instagram filters, no beauty mode, no skin smoothing. The photo must look exactly like you in real life.

Which Countries Accept Home-Taken Digital Photos?

Almost every country does:

  • US: State Department accepts digital photos for online renewal
  • UK: HM Passport Office accepts digital photos taken at home
  • Schengen/EU: All EU countries accept digital photos for visa applications
  • Canada, Australia, New Zealand: All accept home-taken photos that meet specs
The only exception is if you're applying in person for your very first passport — some offices still require printed photos, but you can still print the photo you took at home, you don't need to go to a photo booth.
