Why SHEIN is different from other e-commerce platforms

SHEIN's entire user base shops on mobile, scrolling through a vertical TikTok-style feed. That means 3:4 vertical images fill the entire phone screen, while 1:1 square images leave huge black bars on top and bottom, which makes shoppers scroll past instantly. Internal SHEIN seller data shows listings with 3:4 vertical main images have a 42% higher click-through rate than square images.

For apparel categories, SHEIN does NOT require white backgrounds. In fact, flat lay product shots on white backgrounds perform 28% worse than on-model lifestyle photos. White backgrounds are only required for accessories, jewelry, and home goods categories. This is a big difference from Amazon, Temu, and TikTok Shop, which all mandate pure white main images.

The 8-image listing formula that converts

Top-performing SHEIN listings use all 8 allowed image slots:

  1. Main cover: on-model shot, 3:4 vertical, no text overlays
  2. Close-up detail shot of fabric texture
  3. Detail shot of stitching, hardware, or tags
  4. Back view of the outfit on model
  5. Size reference shot: model holding a tape measure or next to a standard object
  6. Size chart graphic with measurements in inches and cm
  7. Lifestyle shot: model wearing the outfit out and about
  8. Packaging/what's-in-the-box shot
Listings with fewer than 4 images consistently underperform by 30% or more.

How image choice impacts SHEIN conversion rates

SHEIN shoppers are used to scrolling through hundreds of looks in a minute, so your main cover photo is the only chance you have to stop someone from swiping past your listing. Unlike Amazon, where shoppers search for a specific product and compare a handful of options, SHEIN's feed is algorithmic and discovery-driven — people aren't searching for your dress, they're scrolling, and they decide in half a second if they're going to tap based on the main image. That's why 3:4 vertical on-model photos work so much better: they fill the entire phone screen, show the fit of the garment on a real body, and feel like the same TikTok-style content people came to SHEIN to browse. Square product shots feel like a boring Amazon listing in an app built for fast, visual scrolling, and shoppers automatically skip past them.

Another thing new SHEIN sellers get wrong is over-editing their photos. It's tempting to crank up the brightness and saturation to make the colors pop, but that leads to a flood of returns when customers receive the garment and it doesn't look like the photo. SHEIN's review system tracks return rates closely, and if too many customers send items back saying "the color didn't match the photo," your listings get suppressed in search. Keep the color editing minimal: correct white balance so the colors look true to life, don't oversaturate, and shoot in natural daylight so the fabric color looks exactly like it does in person. It feels counterintuitive, but slightly muted, accurate photos lead to fewer returns, higher seller ratings, and better long-term ranking than hyper-saturated, edited photos that look nothing like the real product.

For sellers who are just starting out and can't afford professional on-model photography, you don't need a full studio setup. You can shoot outfit flat lays against a clean wall, or even use a mannequin, as long as the final image is 3:4 vertical and well-lit. What matters most is that the image is sharp, well-lit, and shows the garment clearly — shoppers on SHEIN expect fast, casual product photos, not high-fashion editorial shoots. A well-lit, vertical shot taken on your phone next to a window will outperform a dark, square, blurry shot taken with an expensive camera every time, because it fits the platform's format and looks like the rest of the content people are already browsing. You don't need a big budget to make compliant, high-performing SHEIN product photos, you just need to follow the 3:4 vertical ratio and make sure the lighting is even and natural.
