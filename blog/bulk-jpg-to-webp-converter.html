Why WebP matters for your site

Google explicitly recommends WebP as the best image format for the web. Smaller images mean:

  • Better Core Web Vitals scores (LCP, INP, CLS), which directly boost search rankings
  • Less bandwidth usage, which lowers your Cloudflare or hosting bill on high-traffic days
  • Faster mobile load times, which reduces bounce rate on mobile visitors who are 70%+ of most ecommerce store traffic
  • Support for transparency (like PNGs) at half the file size — great for logos and product cutouts
The problem? Most online JPG to WebP converters force you to upload every image to their server, cap batches at 10 files, and re-compress your images to tiny sizes. That's terrible if you're converting 100+ product photos for your store, and it creates an unnecessary privacy risk for unreleased products.

Common mistakes to avoid

  1. Setting quality too low: If you set WebP quality below 75%, product photos will look washed out and banded. 80-85% is the sweet spot — you get 30% smaller files with zero visible quality loss, even when customers zoom in.
  2. Forgetting to update your image paths: After converting, don't just drop the new .webp files in your folder and hope for the best — update all image src tags in your HTML to point to the new .webp files. This is the most common mistake people make, and it leads to broken images everywhere.
  3. Using cloud converters for private images: If you're converting customer photos, internal screenshots, or unreleased product assets, uploading them to a random online tool is a privacy risk. Local processing means no data ever leaves your device.
  4. Converting original masters: Always keep your original JPG/PNG master files. WebP is great for the live site, but if you ever need to re-edit a product photo, you want to start from the highest quality original, not a compressed WebP.

When should you bulk convert to WebP?

  • Optimizing an old site: If your site has been live for a year with hundreds of unoptimized JPGs, batch converting them all is the fastest way to lift your Core Web Vitals score overnight.

What quality setting actually looks good on product photos?

One of the most common questions people have when converting JPGs to WebP is what quality setting to use, and the answer depends on what you're using the images for. For most e-commerce product photos, 82-85% quality is the sweet spot: at that setting, WebP compresses the file by 30-35% compared to the original JPG, but you can't tell the difference at normal viewing size, even when customers zoom in on your product details. If you go below 75%, you'll start to see banding on smooth gradients, especially on product photos with solid color backgrounds, and text or fine details on your product will start to look soft. If you go above 90%, the file size savings become almost negligible — you might only cut the file size by 5-10%, which isn't worth the conversion effort. For blog post images and screenshots, you can drop the quality down to 75%, because those images don't need to look as crisp as product photos, and the smaller file size helps your blog load faster. It's worth testing a few settings on one sample product photo first, zooming in to check fine details, and picking the highest compression that still looks perfect — that way you don't waste space on images that look worse than they need to.

Another thing people miss when converting to WebP is that you don't actually have to remove all your JPGs entirely. Most modern websites use the picture tag to serve WebP to browsers that support it, and fall back to JPG for the tiny share of users on very old browsers (less than 2% of visitors in 2026). That means you keep both versions of each image on your server: the WebP version for 98% of visitors, and the original JPG for the small share of people on old devices. The extra storage cost is negligible, because the WebP version is so much smaller than the original JPG, and you never have to worry about a visitor getting a broken image. A lot of people are scared to convert to WebP because they think they'll break their site for old visitors, but the fallback method is simple, and it's what every major website (including YouTube, Facebook, and Amazon) uses to serve images fast, without leaving anyone behind.

For people running Shopify or WooCommerce stores, there's good news: most modern e-commerce themes automatically serve WebP versions of your images if you upload them alongside your JPGs, so you don't have to edit any code. You just upload both the .jpg and .webp versions of each product photo to your product folder, and the theme automatically picks the right format for each visitor. If you're on Shopify, their CDN even does this automatically for you — you don't have to convert anything at all, their servers serve optimized WebP versions of your images on the fly. But if you're running a custom static site or a self-hosted WooCommerce store without optimization plugins, bulk converting all your existing images is a 30-minute job that will give you an immediate, noticeable speed boost. It's one of the highest-ROI SEO changes you can make, because image optimization is the easiest part of Core Web Vitals to fix, and it gives you an immediate ranking boost without any other changes to your site.

One last tip: when you batch convert your images, keep the file names exactly the same as the original JPGs, just change the extension to .webp. That makes updating your HTML or CMS much easier, because you can do a simple find-and-replace across all your templates to swap .jpg for .webp, without having to rename every single image file. It also means you won't accidentally break links when you migrate, because the file path stays identical. And always keep your original master JPGs in a separate folder on your hard drive, so if you ever need to re-edit a photo, adjust the crop, or re-export at a different size, you have the high-quality original to work from, instead of starting over from a compressed WebP. It takes almost no extra disk space to keep the originals, and it saves you from having to re-shoot product photos later if you decide to change your store layout. Once you build this conversion step into your product upload workflow, your site will stay fast and optimized forever, no last-minute panic when your Lighthouse score drops.
