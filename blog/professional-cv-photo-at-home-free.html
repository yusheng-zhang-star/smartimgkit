What Makes a CV Photo Look Professional?

A standard professional CV photo requires: 2x2 inch (or 35x45mm) size, plain white/light gray background, neutral facial expression, even lighting, business casual clothing, and the head takes up 60-70% of the frame. Most people mess this up at home: bad lighting, messy backgrounds, wrong size, awkward angles.

Tips for taking a CV photo at home that actually looks professional

The whole "take a CV photo at home" idea sounds easy, but there are a few small mistakes people make that make the final result look obviously DIY instead of studio-quality. The first is lighting: stand facing a window with natural light coming straight at you, not from the side or behind. Side lighting creates harsh shadows on one half of your face, and backlighting turns you into a dark silhouette — both are instant red flags on a professional CV. You don't need fancy ring lights: a plain white wall next to a window on a cloudy day gives perfectly even, soft light that looks exactly like studio softboxes. If you have to shoot at night, turn on every overhead light in the room to make sure your face is evenly lit, and avoid standing under a single warm desk lamp, which casts yellow, unflattering shadows under your eyes and chin.

The second common mistake is angle. Hold your phone at eye level, straight on, not tilted up or down. Tilt the camera down and your face looks like you're looking up from below, which is unflattering and makes your chin look heavier; tilt it up and you look like you're staring down your nose at the recruiter, which comes off as arrogant. Rest your phone on a stack of books on a table across from you to get the perfect eye-level height without needing a tripod, then set the self-timer so you don't have to hold the phone and shake the frame. You want your shoulders square to the camera, your eyes looking directly into the lens, and a small, neutral smile — not a full grin, not a serious scowl, just the kind of expression you'd have greeting a coworker in the hallway.

One underrated advantage of making your CV photo at home instead of going to a studio is that you can take 50 shots in 10 minutes and pick the one where you actually look like yourself. When you go to a studio, you get one or two takes, the photographer tells you to smile, and you end up with a photo that feels stiff and unnatural — the kind of photo that doesn't even look like you to your own friends. When you shoot at home, you can relax, adjust your shirt, fix your hair, and keep shooting until you get a frame where your expression feels natural and approachable. Recruiters look at hundreds of CVs a day, and a photo where you look relaxed and genuine stands out far more than a stiff, over-staged studio headshot that looks like every other candidate's.

For people applying for jobs in more conservative industries — finance, law, government — the standard rules still apply: solid dark blazer, neutral collared shirt, no loud patterns, no visible piercings or tattoos if your company culture is traditional. For startups, creative roles, or tech positions, business casual is perfectly fine, and you don't need to overthink it. The whole point of the CV photo isn't to show off your fashion sense, it's to give the recruiter a friendly, professional face to put to your name, so they remember your application when they're sorting through a pile of identical PDFs. As long as you look like someone they'd want to grab coffee with in the office, you've done it right.
