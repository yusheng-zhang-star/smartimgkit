Common Shopify image mistakes

  • Blurry zoom images: Happens when your source image is smaller than 800×800px. Shopify automatically stretches it to fit the theme's image container, and it gets pixelated when customers hover to zoom. Fix: resize all images to at least 1200×1200px before uploading.
  • Slow store load time: Happens when you upload 10MB RAW files straight from your camera. Shopify doesn't compress them aggressively, and your product page takes 8+ seconds to load — which costs you mobile sales. Fix: compress to under 4MB WebP before uploading.
  • Inconsistent cropping: Some sellers upload portrait, landscape, and square images mixed together, and the product gallery looks messy on desktop and mobile. Fix: crop all your product photos to 1:1 square ratio before uploading — use our bulk square cropper to do this in one batch.
  • Transparent background PNGs that are too big: If you have cut-out product images on white, export as WebP instead of PNG — you get the same transparent result, but the file size is 70% smaller.

Which format should I use?

For 99% of product photos, WebP is the best choice — it's 30-50% smaller than JPG at identical visual quality, which cuts your page load time and improves mobile conversion. Only use PNG when you have a product with a transparent background that needs to show through. JPG is fine if you're supporting really old browsers, but Shopify automatically serves WebP to modern visitors anyway.

How image size actually impacts your Shopify sales

It's easy to think product image size is just a technical detail, but it directly affects two things Google and Shopify care about a lot: page speed, and conversion rate. Every extra second your product page takes to load on mobile, you lose roughly 7% of potential customers — and most Shopify stores that are slow have nothing wrong with their theme or hosting, they just uploaded giant, uncompressed product photos that take forever to download on a 4G connection. When you compress all your images to under 2MB WebP and stick to 2048px max resolution, your page load time drops from 6-8 seconds to under 2 seconds, and you'll see your mobile conversion rate climb almost immediately, without changing anything else on your store. That's free revenue — you don't need to run ads or change your prices, you just fix the image sizes and the load speed improves all on its own.

The other hidden benefit of consistent, properly sized images is that it makes your store feel way more professional. When every product photo is the exact same size, perfectly cropped to square, and loads instantly, shoppers subconsciously trust your brand more, even if they can't put their finger on why. Stores where some photos are blurry, some are stretched, some load slowly, and some are cropped weird on mobile feel like hobby projects, even if the products themselves are high quality. For new Shopify stores that don't have thousands of reviews yet, that subconscious trust is everything — you only get one chance to make a first impression, and a slow, blurry storefront will lose sales before a visitor even reads your product description.

One question that comes up all the time is whether to use portrait images for products like clothing or wall art. Shopify does support portrait and landscape images, but mixing them creates a weird, disjointed gallery look on mobile — some images will be tall, some will be square, and the thumbnails in your collection grid will all be different sizes, which looks unprofessional. Even if you're selling shirts or framed art, crop the main product shot to a 1:1 square, and add extra angles as additional images in the gallery. The main thumbnail is the one shoppers see first in search and on collection pages, so making it consistently square makes your whole storefront look tidier and more polished. You can still show the product in use in additional gallery images, but keep the main hero image square and consistently sized across every product in your store. It's a small detail, but it makes a huge difference in how polished your brand feels to first-time visitors.
