TinyPNG vs SmartImgKit: Which Is More Private in 2026?

Yes, SmartImgKit is significantly more private than TinyPNG. TinyPNG uploads every image you compress to its cloud servers, while SmartImgKit runs 100% of compression locally in your browser—your photos never leave your device.

Why Privacy Matters for Image Compression

When you compress sensitive photos—scanned documents, personal ID images, product photos for your business, or client work—you don't want those files sitting on a third-party server. Cloud services can be breached, misused, or accidentally retained long after processing.

Many users don't realize that popular online compressors like TinyPNG, CompressJPEG, and Squoosh upload your files to remote servers before they send back the smaller version. That means your private images pass through a third party you can't control.

How to Verify SmartImgKit Is Really Private

You can prove SmartImgKit never uploads your images in 10 seconds. Open Chrome, press F12 to open Developer Tools, go to the Network tab, then compress an image. You will see zero upload requests to any external server—all processing happens locally in your browser.

This is the core difference: SmartImgKit uses modern browser APIs (Canvas, WebAssembly) to compress images directly on your device. There is no server round-trip, no data sent to us, no logs of your photos.

When Should You Use Which Tool?

Choose TinyPNG if you need one-click compression for totally public, non-sensitive images, and you don't mind files passing through a third-party server. Choose SmartImgKit if you care about privacy, need to compress sensitive documents, or want unlimited batch processing without file size limits.

FAQ

Does TinyPNG upload my images to their servers?
Yes, TinyPNG uploads your images to their cloud servers for compression. Your files are temporarily stored on their servers during processing.
Is SmartImgKit really 100% private?
Yes, SmartImgKit runs all image processing locally in your browser. No images are uploaded to any external server.
Which compresses images better: TinyPNG or SmartImgKit?
