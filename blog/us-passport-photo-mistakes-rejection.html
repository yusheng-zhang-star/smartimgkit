10 US Passport Photo Mistakes That Get Your Application Rejected (And How to Fix Them)

Published October 2, 2026 · 8 min read · 1,350 words

Over 20% of US passport applications are rejected on the first try — and most rejections come down to avoidable photo mistakes. The U.S. Department of State enforces strict photo requirements, and even a tiny shadow, a slight head tilt, or glasses glare can put your application on hold for weeks.

This guide walks through the 10 most common passport photo rejection reasons, why each one fails, and exactly how to fix it at home before you submit. No need to pay for a studio — most issues can be corrected with a free browser-based tool.

1. Background Is Not Pure White

Why it gets rejected

The background must be plain white or off-white. Any texture, pattern, wall color, or gradient counts as a failure. Even a slightly gray wall will be flagged.

How to fix it

Upload your photo to SmartImgKit's passport photo tool. It automatically removes the original background and replaces it with pure white — all processing happens in your browser, so your photo never leaves your device.

2. Shadows Behind Your Head

Why it gets rejected

A shadow cast on the background wall violates the "evenly lit plain background" rule. This is the #1 most common rejection reason for at-home photos.

How to fix it

Stand at least 2 meters away from the wall when taking the photo. Use front-facing lighting rather than side lighting. If your photo already has a shadow, the background replacement tool removes it automatically.

3. Head Not Centered or Wrong Proportion

Why it gets rejected

Your head must be exactly centered, with the chin-to-crown measurement between 25 and 35 mm (1 to 1 3/8 inches). If your face is too small, too large, or off to one side, it fails.

How to fix it

Crop to a 1:1 square. Use the passport photo tool's built-in cropping guides to center your face. The tool automatically adjusts to the 2x2 inch US standard.

4. Wearing Glasses

Why it gets rejected

Since 2016, the U.S. Department of State has banned glasses in passport photos — even prescription glasses. Glare on lenses is the main concern.

How to fix it

Take off your glasses for the photo. If you have a medical condition that requires glasses, you need a signed statement from your doctor. No exceptions otherwise.

5. Smiling With Teeth

Why it gets rejected

Passport photos require a neutral expression. Showing your teeth in a smile is considered not neutral, even if it's a natural smile.

How to fix it

Relax your face. Look straight into the camera with both eyes open. A closed-mouth neutral expression is safe. Practice in a mirror before shooting.

6. Bad Lighting or Red Eye

Why it gets rejected

Uneven lighting, dark shadows under the eyes, or red-eye effect all fail. The photo must show natural skin tones with no hot spots.

How to fix it

Use natural daylight from a window, facing the light directly. Avoid camera flash which causes red-eye. If your photo is slightly dark, use the brightness slider in the photo enhancer tool before cropping.

7. Low Resolution or Blurry Image

Why it gets rejected

Digital photos must be at least 600x600 pixels. Blurry, pixelated, or compressed images are rejected immediately.

How to fix it

Use the rear camera on your phone, not the front selfie camera. Export the original uncompressed file. SmartImgKit exports at full resolution — no compression.

8. Wearing Hats or Headbands

Why it gets rejected

Any head covering that is not for religious or medical reasons is prohibited. This includes hats, beanies, headbands, and hair accessories that cover the hairline.

How to fix it

Remove all headwear for the photo. If you wear a hijab or turban for religious reasons, make sure your full face is visible from hairline to chin with no shadows.

9. Selfie or Distorted Perspective

Why it gets rejected

Selfies taken at arm's length have wide-angle distortion that stretches your face. The camera must be at eye level, 1.5 meters away.

How to fix it

Ask someone else to take the photo, or use a tripod with a timer. Camera should be at eye level, straight on — no up-angle or down-angle shots.

10. Wrong Clothing Color

Why it gets rejected

Wearing white or very light-colored clothing can blend into the white background, making it hard to distinguish your shoulders.

How to fix it

Wear a dark-colored top. Avoid uniforms, camouflage, or anything with a pattern. A simple dark crew-neck shirt works best.
