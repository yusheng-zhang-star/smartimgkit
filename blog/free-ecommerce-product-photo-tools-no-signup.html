The Complete Product Photo Workflow

Before you upload any product photo to Amazon, Shopify, or Etsy, you need to run it through this exact workflow:

  1. Remove the background: Cut out the product from the background, put it on pure white. Amazon requires this for main images. Use our background remover.
  2. Resize to platform specs: Amazon wants 2000×2000px, Etsy wants 2700×2000px, Shopify wants 2048×2048px. Use our bulk resizer to batch resize all your photos.
  3. Compress under the file size limit: Most platforms have a 10MB limit, and compressed files upload faster. Use our bulk compressor to get files to 1-3MB.
  4. Add watermarks if needed: If you're sharing photos on social media or worried about image theft, add a subtle watermark with our watermark tool.
  5. Rename files for SEO: Rename image files to include your product keyword (e.g. "cotton-t-shirt-black-m.jpg") instead of IMG_1234.jpg. It helps Google image search. Use our bulk rename tool.
That's the entire workflow. All of these tools are free, no signup, no upload, all running locally in your browser.

Why You Don't Need Photoshop

Most small sellers don't need 90% of what Photoshop does. You don't need layers, masks, brushes, filters, or compositing. You just need to:

  • Cut out the product from the background
  • Resize it to the right dimensions
  • Compress it to under 10MB
  • Rename the file for SEO
That's it. Photoshop costs $10/month, and you'd spend 10 minutes per photo doing these simple tasks. Our toolkit does it in seconds, for free, right in your browser.

  • Don't require any signup or email
  • Don't upload your photos to a server (all processing is local)
  • Don't add watermarks to your output

You can open the tool, process your photos, and close it. No account, no spam, no tracking.

Common Mistakes Sellers Make

  • Skipping the compression step: Uploading 15MB photos to Amazon takes forever, and slow uploads mean you spend all afternoon just uploading. Compress first.
  • Using the wrong size for each platform: Amazon wants square, Etsy wants 3:4 portrait, Shopify wants square. Resize for each platform instead of using the same photo everywhere.
  • Not renaming files: A lot of sellers skip renaming, but SEO-friendly filenames help Google image search find your product photos, which brings free traffic.
  • Using watermarks on main images: Amazon and Etsy don't allow watermarks on main product photos. Save watermarks for lifestyle and social media images.

The 5-minute product photo processing routine

Once you get the workflow down, processing a whole batch of product photos takes almost no time at all. Suppose you just shot 20 new product photos. First, drag all 20 into the background remover, wait 30 seconds for them all to be cut out, and download the transparent PNGs. Next, drag those into the bulk resizer, lock the dimensions to your main platform (say, 2000×2000 square for Amazon), and let it process all 20 at once. Then run the resized photos through the compressor at 75% quality, which brings each one from 4MB to about 1MB. Finally, run the bulk rename tool to name each file with your product keyword. The entire batch — 20 photos — takes about 5 minutes total. Before you had this workflow, you'd spend an hour doing the same tasks one by one in Photoshop, saving each file manually, guessing at compression levels. The time saved adds up fast: if you list 10 new products a week, that's 5 hours a month you get back to spend on photography, marketing, or actually making your product instead of fighting with image software.

No watermarks, no file limits, no uploads of your confidential new product shots, just fast, free processing. You should also make sure you're not accidentally double-compressing images: every time you re-save a JPG, you lose a little bit of quality, so run the resize step before compression, and don't export the same photo twice through the compressor. Keep your original raw product photos in a separate folder, and only ever edit and compress copies — that way if you need to go back and reprocess a photo for a different platform, you're starting from the original high-quality file, not a degraded already-compressed version. A lot of sellers ruin their product photos by re-saving them over and over in different tools, which leaves them looking soft and blurry by the time they get uploaded to Amazon, even though the original photo was sharp. Building a simple folder structure (original shots → processed cutouts → resized final files → compressed final uploads) takes 2 minutes to set up, and it prevents that kind of quality loss entirely. Once you have that workflow dialed in, processing product photos stops feeling like a chore, and you can focus on actually taking good photos instead of fighting with complicated editing software.
