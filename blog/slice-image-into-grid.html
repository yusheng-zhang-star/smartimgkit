Slice an Image Into a Grid (Free, Browser-Based)

Published: September 10, 2026 · 5 min read

Want to make that giant Instagram grid post where 9 small photos line up to make one big image? Or split a product photo into pieces for a collage? You used to need Photoshop to do this, but our free grid slicer splits any image into equal grid pieces right in your browser, no upload, no signup.

How to Slice an Image Into a Grid

  1. Upload the image you want to split to our grid slicer.
  2. Choose how many rows and columns you want: 3x3 for Instagram grid posts, 2x2 for product collages, 4x4 for puzzle pieces.
  3. The tool automatically splits your image into equal-sized pieces, aligned perfectly.
  4. Download all pieces as a ZIP file, named in order (1_1.png, 1_2.png, 2_1.png, etc.).
Your image never gets uploaded to a server — all slicing happens locally in your browser. That means even your unreleased product photos stay private.

Common Use Cases

  • Instagram grid posts: Split one big product image into 9 pieces (3x3), upload them to your Instagram profile in order, and your profile grid looks like one giant image. This gets way more profile visits than regular single posts.
  • Product collages: Split a product photo into a 2x2 grid and add text on each piece to show different features. Perfect for Pinterest pins and social media posts.
  • Puzzle pieces: Split a photo into 12 or 16 pieces to make a custom puzzle. Great for marketing promotions.
  • Website banners: Split a wide banner image into smaller pieces to speed up page loading.

Tips for Perfect Grid Posts

  • Use a square image: Instagram grid posts work best with a square (1:1) image. If your image is rectangular, the grid pieces won't line up correctly when you post them.
  • Upload in the right order: Instagram posts go to the right and down, so you upload the top-left piece first, then go across the top row, then the middle row, then the bottom row. If you upload them out of order, the grid will be scrambled.
  • Leave some space in the middle: Don't put the most important part of your product right in the center. That's where the grid lines cut through, and it will look broken.
  • Check alignment: After splitting, open the first and last piece to make sure they're the same size. If they're not, your original image wasn't perfectly square, and you'll need to crop it first.

Common Mistakes

  • Using a non-square image: If your image is 16:9 or 4:3, splitting it into a 3x3 grid will give you rectangular pieces that don't line up on Instagram. Crop to square first.
  • Uploading pieces in the wrong order: This is the #1 mistake people make. The pieces are numbered row by row, left to right. Upload them in order, don't mix them up.
  • Forgetting to crop out the borders: If your original image has a white border, it will show up in every grid piece. Crop the border first before slicing.
  • Using online slicers that upload your image: Most free grid tools upload your image to their server. Our tool runs locally, so your image never leaves your computer.
