Rounded Corners in Word

Word has offered crop-to-shape for many versions, and it is still the quickest way to put a rounded border around a picture inside a document:

  1. Insert the picture. Go to Insert → Pictures and place the image where you want it in the document.
  2. Select the picture. Click it once. The Picture Format tab appears in the ribbon (in older versions it is called just Format).
  3. Open the Crop menu. On the Picture Format tab, click the arrow under Crop — do not click the Crop button itself, which just does rectangular cropping.
  4. Choose Crop to Shape → Rounded Rectangle. The corners are cut immediately with a default radius.
  5. Adjust the radius. A small yellow handle appears on the picture. Drag it toward the center for rounder corners, away for subtler ones. This is the step most people miss — the default radius often looks too strong or too weak for the layout.
  6. Add a border if you want one. Picture Format → Picture Border lets you add a colored outline that follows the rounded shape, which is what most people actually mean by "a rounded border around a picture."

A quicker alternative: the Picture Styles gallery on the same tab includes preset frames, several of which have rounded corners baked in. One click applies the shape plus a border and shadow, but the radius on those presets is fixed — for a custom radius, use Crop to Shape instead.

Rounded Corners in PowerPoint

PowerPoint uses the identical mechanism, which makes sense — the two programs share the same picture engine:

  1. Insert and select the picture on your slide.
  2. Picture Format → Crop → Crop to Shape → Rounded Rectangle. On the ribbon, PowerPoint groups this under Picture Format just like Word.
  3. Drag the yellow handle to taste the corner radius.
  4. For a perfect circle instead, choose Oval under Crop to Shape while holding Shift — or set the picture's height and width to equal values first, then apply the oval crop.

One PowerPoint-specific note: if your end goal is the slide, rounded pictures blend well with rounded content boxes — apply the same corner radius to both (via Shape Format for the box) so the slide looks deliberate rather than mismatched.

Making a Picture Round Instead of Rounded

People searching for rounded corners often actually want a circle — profile photos, team pages, printed directories. In Word or PowerPoint the route is the same menu: Crop to Shape → Oval, with Shift held to constrain it to a perfect circle. If the source photo is not square, the oval comes out as an ellipse, which is the most common complaint with this method — square the image first (set height = width in the Size group) and then crop.

For circular images that live outside a document — website avatars, Discord or forum profiles, app icons — a document is the wrong container. Why Not the Windows Photos App?

A lot of people try to do this in Windows itself and hit a wall. The built-in Photos app crops freely but has no crop-to-shape feature, and Paint neither crops to a rounded rectangle nor exports transparency in a practical way. That is why the search usually ends at Word or PowerPoint — they are the shape-cropping tools people already have installed.

Getting the Rounded Image Out of Office

Rounding corners inside a document is easy; producing a standalone rounded image file is where it gets fiddly:

  • Right-click → Save as Picture. In recent versions of Word and PowerPoint this exports the picture as it appears — rounded corners included — as a PNG with transparent corners. This is the first thing to try.
  • Version caveat. Some older versions export the original uncropped image instead of the rounded result. If your exported file comes back as a full rectangle, your Office version is doing this — no setting fixes it, and repeating the export will not change it.
  • Do not screenshot. Taking a screenshot of the rounded picture works visually but recompresses the entire image at screen resolution — text and faces soften noticeably. It is the quality-floor option.
  • Print and PDF export are fine. Rounded pictures render correctly in print and in PDF export from both programs; the corner shape is applied at display time.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I round the corners of a picture in Word?

Insert the picture, select it, go to the Picture Format tab, click Crop, choose Crop to Shape, and pick the Rounded Rectangle. Drag the yellow handle on the picture to adjust how round the corners are.

How do I make a picture a perfect circle in Word or PowerPoint?

Use Crop to Shape and choose the Oval while holding Shift, or first set the picture's height and width to the same value so the oval crop becomes a circle. For a reusable circular image file, the free Circle Crop tool does it in one click without Office.

Can I round picture corners in the Windows Photos app?

No. The built-in Photos app and Paint have no crop-to-shape feature, which is why this question usually ends in Word or PowerPoint. The fastest standalone alternatives are Office itself or a free browser tool that crops to a shape without any install.

How do I save a rounded-corner picture as a PNG file?

Right-click the rounded picture in Word or PowerPoint and choose Save as Picture — recent versions export the rounded result as a PNG with transparent corners. Some older versions export the original uncropped image instead; if that happens, a browser-based crop tool is the reliable route.

Can I control how round the corners are?

Yes. After applying Crop to Shape, a yellow handle appears on the picture — dragging it inward increases the corner radius, outward decreases it. The preset rounded styles in the Picture Styles gallery use a fixed radius you cannot adjust.

Does rounding the corners reduce image quality?

No. Crop to Shape hides the corners rather than resizing pixels, so everything inside the shape stays at full resolution. Quality loss only comes from the export route — screenshots recompress the whole image, while Save as Picture to PNG does not.
