Quick pro tips for better memes

The best memes aren't the ones with the fanciest editing — they're the ones that capture a specific, relatable moment that people instantly recognize. You don't need to use a viral template; some of the most popular memes are just a random screenshot from a show, a group chat, or a mundane office moment, with a perfectly timed caption. The tool just handles the text placement and font styling — the actual joke comes from you. Keep it dumb, keep it short, and don't overthink it.

  • Keep text short. The best memes are 3-7 words per line. Long, paragraphs of text kill the joke, and they get cut off on small phone screens.
  • Leave space for text. If you're taking your own photo for a meme, leave a 15-20% blank strip at the top and bottom for your caption — it's way easier than trying to squeeze text over a busy face.
  • Use the classic font. The Impact font with a thick black outline is instantly recognizable as a meme — it reads instantly on tiny screens, which is why it's been the standard for 20+ years.
