Ever needed to post a screenshot online, but it has your email address, customer name, or order number visible? Most people just draw a black box over the sensitive info — but that's not safe. Someone can erase that black box and read the original text. Pixelation is the safe way: it permanently destroys the underlying pixels so no one can recover the original information. Our free pixelate tool does this entirely in your browser, no upload.

How to Pixelate Sensitive Information

  1. Upload your image or screenshot to our pixelate tool.
  2. Drag a box over the area you want to hide — a credit card number, an email address, a customer name, a license plate, anything.
  3. Adjust the pixel size: 10px pixels are subtle, 30px pixels are obvious. Bigger pixels are safer — no one can guess what's under them.
  4. Add as many pixelated boxes as you need.
  5. Export the image. The pixelated areas are permanently baked into the image, no one can undo them.
Your image never gets uploaded to a server — all processing happens locally on your device. That means even your sensitive screenshots never leave your computer.

Why Pixelation Is Safer Than Black Bars

Drawing a black rectangle over text looks like it hides the info, but it's not actually secure:

  • Anyone with basic image editing skills can erase that black layer and read the original text underneath.
  • If you export the image as a layered file, the black bar is just a layer that can be deleted.
Pixelation works by replacing every small block of pixels with a single solid color. The original pixel data is gone forever. There's no way to recover what was under the pixelated area. That's the standard way to redact information in legal, medical, and financial documents.

Common Use Cases

  • Posting screenshots online: If you're posting a screenshot of your Seller Central dashboard, your email, store name, and revenue are visible. Pixelate those areas before posting.
  • Customer support tickets: When you send screenshots to support, pixelate customer names, addresses, and order numbers to protect privacy.
  • Sharing data screenshots: If you're posting a sales report or analytics screenshot, pixelate any numbers or customer info you don't want public.
  • Redacting personal info: If you're sharing an ID, invoice, or receipt, pixelate the ID number, address, and signature before sharing.

Tips for Better Redaction

  • Use big pixels: If you use tiny 5px pixels, someone might be able to guess the text from the shape. Use 15-30px pixels so the shape is completely unrecognizable.
  • Check the whole image: Don't just pixelate the obvious info. Check the corners, the URL bar, the taskbar, and any other places personal info might be visible.
  • Export as JPG: If you export as PNG, someone might be able to reverse-engineer the layers. Export as a flat JPG so the pixelation is permanent.
  • Zoom in to check: After pixelating, zoom in on the area to make sure you covered all the text. It's easy to miss a digit or a letter.

Common Mistakes

  • Using black bars instead of pixelation: As we mentioned, black bars are not secure. Use pixelation for anything sensitive.
  • Pixelating too small an area: If you're pixelating a credit card number, make sure you cover the entire number, not just part of it. A partial number is still sensitive.
  • Uploading sensitive screenshots to online tools: Most online pixelate tools upload your screenshot to their server. Use our local tool so your screenshot never leaves your device.
  • Forgetting to pixelate the URL bar: If you're taking a screenshot of a webpage, the URL in the address bar might contain sensitive info. Pixelate that too.

Why local pixelation matters for sensitive screenshots

A lot of people use random online pixelate tools they find through Google, without realizing what happens to their screenshot once they upload it. Most of those free tools run on a remote server: you upload your screenshot to their service, their server pixelates it, and sends the cleaned version back. That means your sensitive screenshot — which might contain customer data, your revenue numbers, your login screen, or internal business information — is stored on that third-party server, where the tool operator can see it, save it, or leak it in a data breach. For business users who regularly share screenshots of dashboards, customer tickets, or internal reports, that's an enormous compliance risk, especially if you handle customer data and are bound by privacy regulations like GDPR or CCPA. When the pixelation runs locally in your own browser, the screenshot never leaves your machine, there's no third party that can see your sensitive information, and you're fully compliant with basic data handling rules. It's not a small detail — uploading a screenshot with customer PII to a random online tool can get you fined thousands of dollars, if that data gets exposed.

One of the most common redaction mistakes people make is thinking a solid black bar is "good enough" for casual posts. It might be, if you're just posting a meme and the text under the bar is something silly, but for any screenshot with real sensitive information, black bars are actively dangerous. There are countless real cases where people posted screenshots they thought were redacted with black bars, only to have someone on Reddit undo the black bar in 30 seconds using Photoshop, revealing the original credit card number, home address, or private message. It only takes one person with basic editing skills to undo that, and once the information is public, you can't take it back. Pixelation, on the other hand, permanently destroys the original pixels — there's no undo button, no layer to delete, no way to recover what's under the block. That's why every court, hospital, and bank uses pixelation (or true redaction) instead of black boxes for sensitive documents: it's the only method that actually works, long term.
