Official Passport Photo Requirements

While exact requirements vary by country, most share these common rules:

  • Background: Plain white or off-white, no patterns, shadows, or objects. The US requires plain white; many other countries accept light gray or blue.
  • Lighting: Even, diffused lighting with no shadows on the face or background. Natural daylight from a window is ideal. Avoid flash (causes red-eye and harsh shadows).
  • Composition: Head centered, facing the camera directly. Eyes open and visible. Head should fill 50-70% of the photo height (varies by country).
  • Expression: Neutral expression, mouth closed. No smiling (US and most countries). Some countries allow a natural smile.
  • Eyes: Must be open and clearly visible. No hair covering eyes. No glasses (US requires no glasses unless medically necessary, with a signed statement).
  • Clothing: Everyday clothing. No uniforms, costumes, or camouflage. No hats or head coverings (except religious, with a signed statement). No headphones or wireless devices.
  • Quality: In focus, high resolution, no pixelation or blurriness. No filters or retouching that changes appearance. No red-eye.

Step-by-Step Tutorial

  1. Find good lighting. Stand facing a window with natural daylight. The light should be even on both sides of your face. Avoid backlighting (window behind you) and overhead lighting (causes shadows under eyes).
  2. Prepare the background. Stand against a plain white wall. If your wall is not white, hang a white sheet or poster board. Make sure there are no shadows, patterns, or objects in the background.
  3. Take the photo. Have someone take the photo for you (selfies are not accepted). The camera should be at eye level, about 1.5-2 meters away. Look directly at the camera with a neutral expression and mouth closed. Take 3-5 photos and choose the best one.

Pro Tips for Approval

💡

Natural light is best

Take your photo near a large window during the day. Natural daylight is the most flattering and even. Avoid fluorescent lighting (casts green tint) and direct flash (causes red-eye and harsh shadows).

👕

Wear contrasting colors

Wear a dark or colored shirt that contrasts with the white background. Avoid white or light-colored shirts (blend into background). Avoid patterns, logos, or text on clothing.

📏

Check head size

Your head (from top of hair to bottom of chin) should fill 50-70% of the photo height. If too small, move closer or zoom in. If too large, move farther away. The tool provides guidelines to help you get it right.

🚫

No filters or retouching

Do not use beauty filters, skin smoothing, or any retouching that changes your appearance. Passport photos must accurately represent your current appearance. Minor exposure/contrast adjustments are acceptable, but no face-altering filters.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Selfies. Selfies are not accepted for passport photos. The arm holding the phone creates an unnatural angle and the photo may be too close. Always have someone else take the photo.
  • Smiling. The US and most countries require a neutral expression with mouth closed. A big smile can cause your photo to be rejected. Practice a neutral expression in the mirror before taking the photo.
  • Glasses. The US no longer allows glasses in passport photos unless medically necessary (with a signed doctor statement). If you wear glasses every day, take them off for the photo. Avoid transition lenses (may darken in bright light).
  • Shadows on background. If you stand too close to the wall, your shadow will be visible in the background. Stand about 0.5-1 meter away from the wall to avoid shadows. Use a fill light or reflector if needed.
  • Low resolution. Do not take the photo with a low-quality camera or crop a small portion of a larger photo. The final image should be at least 600x600 pixels (US requirement). Use the highest resolution setting on your camera.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I really take my own passport photo at home?

Yes. As long as you follow the official requirements (lighting, background, composition, expression), a home-taken photo is accepted. Many people successfully use home-taken passport photos. The key is to use good natural lighting and a plain white background, and to follow the composition guidelines carefully.

What if my photo is rejected?

If your passport application is rejected due to the photo, you will need to submit a new photo. There is usually no additional fee for resubmitting the photo, but it will delay your application. To avoid rejection, carefully follow the requirements and use the Passport Photo Maker tool to ensure correct dimensions and composition.

Can I smile in my passport photo?

The US and most countries require a neutral expression with mouth closed. A natural, subtle smile may be accepted in some countries, but to be safe, use a neutral expression. Practice in the mirror before taking the photo. The most common reason for photo rejection is an unacceptable expression.

Do I need to print on special paper?

Passport photos should be printed on photo-quality paper (matte or glossy). Regular printer paper is not acceptable because it is too thin and the ink may smudge. You can print at any photo printing service (drugstore, pharmacy, online photo service) for about $0.25-$1 per 4x6 inch print.

How long is a passport photo valid?

A passport photo should reflect your current appearance. If your appearance has changed significantly (new haircut, weight change, facial tattoos, etc.), you should take a new photo. Most countries require that the photo be taken within the last 6 months. When renewing your passport, you must submit a new photo (you cannot reuse the photo from your old passport).
