Pinterest Pin Size Guide: Why 1000×1500 Still Wins in 2026

Published: 2026-10-18 · 9 min read

Vertical 2:3 pins at 1000×1500px take about twice the feed space of a square or horizontal image, and Pinterest's algorithm deprioritizes the latter — which is why repurposing Instagram squares or blog banners is the most common new-creator mistake. Pins also outlive posts: a well-made pin keeps driving clicks and saves for months or years rather than 48 hours. This guide covers the exact 2026 dimensions for standard, idea, and video pins.

Pinterest is still the most underrated traffic source for creators and small ecommerce brands. Unlike Instagram or TikTok, where posts disappear from feeds in 48 hours, a well-made Pinterest pin can drive clicks and saves for months or even years after you post it. We've seen pins from 2024 still sending 100+ visits a month, with zero ongoing effort. But that only happens if you get the size and format right.

The #1 mistake new Pinterest creators make: uploading square Instagram photos or horizontal blog banner images directly as pins. These get half the feed real estate of a properly sized vertical pin, and the algorithm deprioritizes them. The good news: the ideal pin size hasn't changed in years, and it's extremely easy to get right.

A lot of creators overthink their pin designs, spending hours on Canva making elaborate graphics with fancy fonts, gradients, and decorative elements — but the pins that actually perform best are surprisingly simple. High-contrast, bold text overlay on top of a clear, high-quality photo works better than any fancy graphic design, because people scroll Pinterest on their phones at 60mph, and they need to understand what your pin is about in less than half a second. If your pin requires zooming in to read small text, or it's visually cluttered, people will scroll right past it. That's why the 2:3 vertical format works so well: you can put your hook headline right in the top third of the image, where it's immediately visible on mobile, and the product/photo fills the rest of the frame. You don't need a design degree to make high-performing pins — you just need the right dimensions, clear large text, and a photo that matches the topic people are searching for. Most small creators who start posting consistently see 1,000+ monthly visitors from Pinterest within 3 months, just by pinning 2-3 properly sized pins a week. It's one of the highest ROI traffic channels for free, and it compounds over time — unlike social media platforms where your posts vanish after a day, Pinterest keeps surfacing your old pins for years, so every pin you make is a tiny permanent traffic source working for you 24/7.

Another common mistake: making pins that are too tall. Pinterest will cut off any image longer than a 1:2 ratio, meaning if you make a 1000×2100 extra-long pin, the bottom third gets chopped off in the feed, and people never see the call to action or the final part of your graphic. Stick strictly to 2:3, 1000×1500, and you'll never get cut off. File size also matters: if your pin is a 5MB uncompressed PNG, it will load slowly on mobile, and Pinterest deprioritizes slow-loading pins in the feed. Compressing to under 500KB as a high-quality JPG means your pin loads instantly, gets shown to more people, and performs better overall. You don't need to upload a giant 4000px wide master file to look good — 1000×1500 is exactly the resolution Pinterest serves on mobile feeds anyway, so anything larger is just wasted file size that slows down your page and hurts performance. Once you have a set of properly sized pins, you can schedule them weeks in advance, and they'll keep driving traffic to your blog or product pages while you work on other things. It's a completely passive traffic channel once you put in the initial work of making a batch of pins, which is why so many small bloggers and ecommerce brands rely on it as their main free traffic source.

The optimal Pinterest pin size in 2026

Pinterest officially recommends a 2:3 aspect ratio, at 1000×1500 pixels. This vertical ratio fills almost the entire mobile screen when someone scrolls through their feed, which means your pin gets seen, read, and saved at 2-3x the rate of square or horizontal images.

There are hard limits you can't ignore:

  • Minimum size: 600×900px. Anything smaller looks pixelated on retina screens.
  • Maximum height: 1500px. If your pin is taller than this — like a 1000×2100 long infographic — Pinterest will cut it off in the feed, and users will only see the top third.

What actually makes a pin perform? (It's not just size)

Getting the dimensions right is the baseline, but the highest-performing pins share three traits that have stayed consistent through every Pinterest algorithm update since 2020:

  1. Bold, readable text overlay. 80% of Pinterest users browse on mobile, and they scroll fast. If someone can't tell what your pin is about in half a second, they scroll past. Use large, high-contrast text: dark text on light backgrounds, or white text on dark gradients. Keep the title short — 3-7 words, no long paragraphs.
  2. Vertical composition that fills the frame. Don't leave huge empty margins at the top and bottom of your pin. The 2:3 ratio is designed to fill the mobile screen; if your product or text only takes up the middle third, you're wasting the biggest advantage of vertical pins.
  3. Searchable keywords in the title and description. Pinterest is a search engine, not just a social network. People type "small apartment kitchen ideas" into the Pinterest search bar, and your pin shows up if your title and alt text include those exact phrases. Don't just write "My new blog post" — write "10 Small Apartment Kitchen Ideas Under $50".

Common pin size mistakes that kill reach

  • Making pins too tall. New creators often make super-long infographic pins at 1:3 or taller, thinking more content = more saves. But Pinterest cuts anything over 1500px tall in the feed, so users only see the top. If you have a long infographic, split it into 2-3 separate 2:3 pins.
  • Using square Instagram photos. Square pins take up 50% less space in the feed than vertical ones. Even if your photo looks great on Instagram, resize it to 2:3 before posting to Pinterest.
  • Uploading uncompressed files. A 5MB JPEG from your camera takes 5 seconds to load on mobile data. Most users scroll away before the image even appears. Compress it to under 500KB first.
  • Putting important text near the edges. Pinterest adds a small "Save" button overlay at the bottom right of every pin, and profile info at the top. Keep your text and product 10% in from all edges to avoid being covered up.
  • Ignoring alt text. Pinterest uses alt text to understand what your pin is about and show it in search. Write a 2-3 sentence description that includes your target keywords, not just "product photo".

FAQ

What is the best Pinterest pin size in 2026? 1000×1500px (2:3 vertical) is still the sweet spot: it fills the feed without being cropped, and 2:3 is the ratio Pinterest itself recommends.

Can I use a square pin? Yes, 1000×1000 works, but a 2:3 pin takes about 60% more vertical space in the feed, which consistently earns more saves and clicks.

What aspect ratios should I avoid? Anything wider than 1:1 gets cropped or pushed down the feed, and extremely tall pins beyond 1:2.63 are truncated with a see-more cut.

Does file size matter for pins? Pinterest's technical limit is 20MB, but a WebP or compressed JPEG under 1MB loads faster and stays sharp on mobile connections.
