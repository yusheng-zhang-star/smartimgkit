Every seller needs to turn web pages into images sometimes: saving a competitor's product page for reference, creating a social media preview of your blog post, documenting a pricing change, or archiving a listing. The built-in Windows/Mac screenshot tool only captures what's visible on screen, and you have to stitch multiple screenshots together. How It Works

  1. Paste any public web URL into the input box.
  2. Our tool loads the page directly in your browser and captures a full-page screenshot — the entire length of the page, not just the visible window.
  3. Choose output format: JPG (smaller file, good for social media) or PNG (sharp, good for documentation).

There's no upload step: the page loads in your own browser, so any pages that require login (like your own Seller Central) will load with your existing login state. It works for any public website.

Why Not Use Built-In Screenshot Tools?

The built-in Snipping Tool on Windows or Cmd+Shift+5 on Mac only captures your current viewport. If you're capturing a long blog post or product page, you have to scroll down, take another screenshot, and stitch them together in Photoshop. It takes 5 minutes per page. Our tool captures the entire page in 2 seconds, automatically.

Tips for Better Screenshots

  • Wait for the page to load: If the page has images or lazy-loading content, wait 2 seconds after the page loads before capturing — otherwise the lower parts of the page will be blank.
  • Use PNG for text-heavy pages: If you're capturing a page with lots of text or code, PNG keeps text sharp. JPG blurs text slightly when compressed.
  • Use JPG for photo-heavy pages: If you're capturing a landing page with lots of images, JPG gives you a smaller file size that's easier to share.
  • Scroll down first: If the page has lazy-loaded images, scroll all the way to the bottom first, then scroll back up, then capture — that forces all images to load.

Common Mistakes

  • Trying to capture pages that require login: If the page requires you to log in first, open it in your own browser first, log in, then use the URL capture tool — it will load with your existing session.
  • Capturing pages with ads or popups: If the page has a popup ad blocking content, close the popup first in your browser, then capture the URL.
  • Using the screenshot for copyrighted content: You can use screenshots for internal reference, but don't republish someone else's full page as your own content — that's copyright infringement.

Why local browser capture beats online screenshot APIs

There are a lot of online URL-to-image tools that run on remote servers, but they all have the same big downside: when you paste a URL into them, they send that URL to a third-party server, which loads the page and sends back a screenshot. That means the operator of that tool can see every URL you capture, and if you're capturing internal pages, pricing sheets, or customer data, that sensitive information is passing through an unknown third party. Worse, most of those server-based tools have strict limits: free tiers only let you capture 5 pages a day, they add watermarks to the images, and they often fail on pages that require login or have heavy JavaScript content. When the capture runs locally in your own browser, the page loads on your own machine, no URL leaves your computer, and you can capture as many pages as you want, no limits, no watermarks, no signup. For anyone who handles sensitive business information, that privacy difference isn't a nice-to-have, it's the whole point.
