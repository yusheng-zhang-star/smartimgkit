Why Amazon strictly enforces square white background images

Amazon automatically suppresses product listings that don't meet image requirements, which means your product will not show up in search results or on product detail pages until your images are fixed — even if your listing title and price are perfect. The current Amazon main image requirements are non-negotiable: images must be 1:1 aspect ratio, minimum 1000x1000 pixels, pure white background (RGB 255,255,255), the product must fill at least 85% of the frame, and no text, logos, or watermarks are allowed on the main listing image.

2000x2000 isn't an arbitrary number — it's the minimum resolution that enables Amazon's built-in zoom feature, which lets shoppers hover and see fine details of your product. Internal Amazon data shows listings with zoom-enabled images convert 15-20% better than those without. That's why 2000px is the recommended size, even though Amazon technically allows as small as 1000px.

What about product shadows?

A lot of new sellers think "pure white background" means the product should look like it's floating in empty space, with zero shadow at all. In practice, that flat, shadowless look makes products look cheap and fake, and shoppers subconsciously don't trust them. Amazon's rules actually allow a very soft, subtle drop shadow directly under the product — it just can't be a harsh, obvious shadow that extends far out onto the white background. If you want that professional look, after you generate your 2000x2000 image, you can add a 10-15% opacity soft shadow under the product using the free photo editor, which makes the product feel grounded and real without violating Amazon's white-background rule. Most top Amazon sellers use this trick, and it makes a huge difference in how premium your listing looks, even when you're shooting product photos on a phone at home.

Another thing sellers often get wrong is the file format. Amazon accepts both JPG and PNG for main images, but JPG is almost always the better choice — PNG files are 2-3x larger for product photos, which slows down page load times, and Amazon actually penalizes slow-loading listings in search rankings. The only time you should use PNG is if your product has transparent elements, like clear plastic packaging, glass, or transparent jewelry, where JPG compression would leave ugly color artifacts. For 99% of products, export as a high-quality JPG at 85% quality, which gives you a file size around 200-400KB — small enough to load instantly, but sharp enough that the zoom feature works perfectly. If your file comes out over 2MB, run it through the free image compressor before uploading, because Amazon will reject images over 10MB and very large files slow down your whole listing.
