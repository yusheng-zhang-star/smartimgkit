Which Background Color Do You Need?

Not every country uses the same background. Getting this wrong means your passport application gets rejected. Here is the quick reference:

WHITE

US / UK / Schengen

Plain white or off-white. No shadows. Most common requirement worldwide.

LIGHT BLUE

China / India / Thailand

Medium light blue (#438EDB). Required for Chinese passports and Indian visa photos.

RED

Some ID Cards

Red is used for national ID cards in China and a few other countries, not for passports.

Using the wrong color wastes your application fee and adds weeks of delay. Always double-check your destination country's official requirements before you print.

What to do when the edge cutout looks rough

AI background removal is usually very accurate for head-and-shoulders portraits, but there are a few cases where the cutout comes out rough: if you have messy flyaway hair that blends into the original background, if you're wearing thin wire-rimmed glasses, or if you took the photo in low light where your edges are soft and blurry. The most common sign of a bad cutout is a faint halo of the old background color around your hair or shoulders, which will show up clearly once you put the new white or blue background behind you. If that happens, don't try to fix it by hand in Photoshop — just re-take the original photo with more even lighting against a higher-contrast wall, and run it through the tool again. Better light in the original shot means the AI has clearer edges to work with, and the cutout will come out perfectly clean on the first try, no manual retouching needed.

Another common mistake people make is choosing a background color that's too close to their clothing or hair color. If you have blonde hair and you pick an off-white background, the edges of your hair will blend right into the background, and the consulate scanner will flag it as a low-quality photo. If you have dark hair or wear a dark suit, a white background works perfectly, but if you have very light blonde or gray hair, make sure you're using a pure bright white, not a warm off-white, so your hair has clear contrast against the background. The same rule applies to blue backgrounds: if you're wearing a bright blue shirt, pick a slightly different shade of blue for the ID background, so your shirt doesn't blend into the backdrop. It's a small detail, but it's one of the most common reasons home-taken ID photos get rejected, even when the background color is technically correct.

For people who wear religious head coverings, the background changer works perfectly, as long as you take the original photo with even lighting on both sides of the covering. The AI will automatically cut around the edges of the head covering without cutting into your hair or fabric, and the new solid background will look clean and professional. Just make sure no shadows fall on the fabric, because shadows on the head covering are one of the most common rejection reasons for religious ID photos, and no background changer can fix a shadow that's baked into the original photo itself. Standing facing a window, with light evenly illuminating both sides of your face and head covering, gives the AI a clean cutout to work with, and you won't have any rough edges to fix.
