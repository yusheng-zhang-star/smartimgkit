Exact vs near-duplicate photos: what's the difference?

Most built-in operating system tools only find exact duplicates: files with identical file hashes, meaning they're literally the same file copied multiple times. That's not what sellers actually need. In real product photo workflows, duplicates almost never have the same filename — they're near-duplicates: the same image, but slightly modified. The tool finds all of these:

  • Same image, different filenames. You saved the same supplier image as `mug-main.jpg`, then exported it as `mug-etsy-2000.jpg`, then downloaded it again from the supplier drive as `mug-draft.jpg`. They're the same pixels, different names.
  • Same image, different resolutions. You have the original 4000×4000 product photo, and the 2000×2000 resized version you uploaded to Amazon. These take up twice the space they need to, and you don't need both versions saved locally if you keep the high-res original.
  • Same image, different compression. You exported one version at 80% quality, another at 60% quality, for different platform uploads. They look identical at a glance, but they're two separate files.
  • Slightly cropped versions. You cropped 10% off the edge for Instagram, saved it as a new file, and now you have two nearly identical versions taking up space.

The tool compares the actual visual pixels of each photo, not filenames. It doesn't care what the file is called, or what size it is — if two images look the same, it groups them together.

  1. Open the tool in your browser. It works in Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari — no install required.

Why local scanning beats cloud duplicate finders

Almost every "free online duplicate photo finder" you find on Google works like this: you upload your entire photo folder to their server, their AI scans for duplicates, then they show you the results. That sounds convenient, but it has two huge problems:

First, privacy. Your unreleased product photos, supplier assets, and any personal photos mixed in with your business files are sent to an unknown third-party server. You have no idea who can access them, or how long they're stored. For product photos that haven't launched yet, that's a real leak risk.

Second, speed. Uploading 10GB of product photos to a cloud server takes hours, even on fast internet. Local scanning takes 60 seconds for the same folder, because it never leaves your computer.

Our tool does all the visual comparison on your device using your browser's built-in image processing. It's fast, private, and you don't have to trust any third party with your files.

Common duplicate sources for ecommerce sellers

  • Supplier asset packs. You download the same product photos from Alibaba or your supplier multiple times, across different email threads and chat messages, never realizing you already have them.
  • Export variants. You export one version for Etsy, one for Amazon, one for TikTok Shop, each with slightly different dimensions, and save all three locally.
  • Edit iterations. You edit a product photo in Canva, save it, tweak the brightness, save again as a new version, and end up with 5 almost identical versions in your folder.
  • Phone imports. You import product photos from your phone twice — once to your main folder, once to an "unsorted" folder — and they never get merged.

Most sellers don't realize how much duplicate clutter they have until they run a scan. It's normal to find 20-30% of your photo folder is just duplicates, taking up space for no reason.

Post-scan cleanup workflow

Once you've deleted duplicates, set up a simple system to stop duplicates from building up again:

  • Keep only the original high-resolution product photos in your main folder. Resized versions are generated on the fly when you upload to marketplaces, you don't need to save them locally.
  • Name files consistently: `product-name-main.jpg`, `product-name-2.jpg`, etc. Skip the `final`, `v2`, `final-final` naming chaos.
  • Run the duplicate finder once a quarter, after big photoshoots, to catch new duplicates before they pile up.
