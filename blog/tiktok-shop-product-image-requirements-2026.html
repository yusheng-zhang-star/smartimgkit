Why TikTok Shop is different from Amazon and Temu

Most sellers come to TikTok Shop from Amazon or Temu, and they assume the rules are the same. They're close, but there are a few key differences that trip people up:

  • TikTok cares more about how your image looks tiny in the feed. Amazon shoppers are already searching for your product; TikTok shoppers are scrolling videos and will tap your listing only if the thumbnail pops. That means your product needs to fill 85-90% of the frame, with no tiny product lost in a big white space.
  • TikTok allows lifestyle detail images on slides 2-9, and rewards them. Since TikTok is a short-video platform, slides that show the product in real use — someone wearing your jewelry, using your kitchen gadget — perform way better than sterile white-background detail shots.
  • TikTok is stricter about text on the main image. Even a tiny "NEW" sticker in the corner of the main cover will trigger an automatic rejection, whereas other platforms let you get away with small promo text.

What TikTok absolutely does NOT allow on main images

Any of these on your first image will get your listing rejected, no warnings:

  • Text overlays, sale badges, discount stickers, or promotional graphics
  • Watermarks, brand logos, or your store name printed on the product itself
  • Borders, frames, or colored backgrounds
  • People, hands, props, or lifestyle context — that's what slides 2-9 are for
  • Collage images with multiple products crammed into one frame

You have 8 other slides to show all the context you want. Put the size chart, the lifestyle shot, the detail close-up, and the "what's in the box" graphic on images 2 through 9 — those can be anything. Just keep the first image clean, white, and product-only.

What to put on slides 2 through 9 to get more sales

Since TikTok rewards lifestyle content, the other 8 image slots are where you actually win over shoppers. The first slide gets you past review, but slides 2-9 are what convince someone to buy. The highest-converting slide order we see from top sellers is: slide 2 is a close-up detail shot showing the product texture (the stitching on a wallet, the glitter on a phone case, the fabric weave on a t-shirt), slide 3 is a lifestyle shot of someone actually using the product in real life, slide 4 is a size reference shot next to a common object (like a coffee mug) so shoppers know how big the item actually is, slide 5 is the "what's in the box" shot showing everything that comes in the package, slide 6 is a before/after comparison if it's a beauty or cleaning product, slide 7 is a shot showing the product's packaging, slide 8 is a FAQ graphic answering the most common question about the product, and slide 9 is a simple shot of the product on white again, to close the loop. That structure works because it answers every possible question a shopper might have before they even have to read the product description, which cuts down on support questions and cart abandonment.

A lot of new sellers waste those extra slots on more sterile white-background shots of the same product from slightly different angles, which doesn't help at all. TikTok shoppers don't want to see 9 nearly identical white-background photos — they want to see what the product actually looks like when someone uses it, how big it is, and what they're actually getting when they order. The algorithm also favors listings that keep people swiping through all the images, because longer session time signals to TikTok that your listing is high quality, so it pushes it to more people in the For You Page feed. That's why filling all 9 slots with varied, useful content isn't just a nice-to-have — it's a ranking signal that gets your product in front of more shoppers for free.

One last tip: don't over-edit your product photos. TikTok shoppers are used to seeing raw, authentic phone footage from creators, so if your product photos look like they came from a fancy studio shoot, they can feel out of place in the feed. The main image still needs to be clean and white, but the lifestyle shots on the later slides can look like real photos taken by a creator, not a professionally shot commercial. That authenticity is part of why TikTok Shop works so well — it feels like a friend showing you a product they like, not a corporate ad. Over-polishing your images actually hurts performance on this platform, because it breaks that authentic feel that makes shoppers trust the listing.
