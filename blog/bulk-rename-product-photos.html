Why filenames actually matter for your store

Google reads the filename as part of an image's context, the same way it reads alt text. If your product photo is named "black-leather-bifold-wallet-front.jpg", Google immediately knows what the image is showing, and it will surface that image when people search "black leather bifold wallet" — free, targeted traffic that most sellers never tap into. If it's named "DSC_0019.jpg", Google has no idea what it is, and it will never rank in image search, no matter how good the photo looks.

Consistent naming also makes your daily work way easier:

  • You can instantly tell which photo belongs to which product without opening it, even when you're scrolling through 500 files
  • Your asset library stays organized — no more digging through folders named "new folder new new final 2" looking for the right shot
  • When you re-list products next quarter, update listings for a sale, or send images to a virtual assistant, you know exactly which files to grab
  • It cuts down on support tickets too — if a customer asks for a specific detail shot, you can pull it up in 2 seconds instead of 10 minutes

The best naming convention for ecommerce sellers

Follow these simple rules, and your filenames will work for you instead of against you:

  1. All lowercase, no spaces. Use hyphens instead of spaces: "blue-cotton-tshirt.jpg" works everywhere, "Blue Cotton T-Shirt.jpg" breaks on many web hosts and Amazon uploaders.
  2. Include your main keyword. Use words shoppers actually search for — "wireless-bluetooth-earbuds.jpg", not "earbuds1.jpg". Don't stuff keywords, just describe the product accurately.
  3. Add view numbers at the end. -1 for the main white-background shot, -2 for the side view, -3 for the close-up detail, -4 for the lifestyle shot. It keeps your gallery order straight when you upload.
  4. Skip special characters. No ?, &, #, or emojis — these break file paths on most servers and hosting platforms.
  5. Don't include SKU numbers. The SKU lives in your listing backend, not in the filename. Keywords help SEO, SKUs don't.

What Amazon vs Google actually uses filenames for

There's a common misconception that Amazon uses filenames for its internal search ranking. It doesn't. Amazon's algorithm reads the title, bullet points, and backend search terms — not your image filenames. But consistent naming still matters for Amazon sellers, because it keeps your own workflow organized when you're managing 100+ listings.

Where filenames really move the needle is Google Image Search. If you run a Shopify store, WooCommerce site, or Etsy shop, correctly named product images can drive thousands of extra views a month from image search — traffic that you don't pay for, and that converts at the same rate as regular site traffic. It's the lowest-effort SEO win in ecommerce, and most sellers ignore it completely.

3 common naming mistakes to avoid

  • Using generic names. "Product1.jpg", "Final.jpg", "New version.jpg" — these tell Google nothing, and they'll get lost in your files in 3 months.
  • Putting sizes or colors in the wrong order. "shirt-red-large.jpg" is fine, but "large-red-shirt.jpg" doesn't read naturally. Lead with the product, then the variant.
  • Renaming manually. Right-clicking each file, hitting Rename, typing the name, hitting enter — that's 5 seconds per photo, times 50 photos, equals 250 seconds of mindless clicking. Just use the bulk tool.
