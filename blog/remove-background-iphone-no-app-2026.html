How to Remove Background from iPhone Photos Without Any App (2026 Guide)

Published: September 21, 2026 · 8 min read

How to Remove Backgrounds From iPhone Photos Using Built-in iOS Features (No Apps Needed)

Let me guess what brought you here. You've got a great photo — maybe a product shot for your small business, a portrait you want to use as a profile picture, or just a fun picture of your dog — and you want to cut out the subject and ditch the background. So you did what most people do: you opened the App Store, searched "background remover," and got hit with a wall of subscription pop-ups, watermarks, and "free trials" that expire in three days.

Here's the good news: you don't need any of that. Your iPhone can remove backgrounds natively, and it's been able to do so since iOS 16. Whether you're on iOS 17, iOS 18, or the newer iOS 26, Apple's built-in tools handle the job surprisingly well — and they're completely free.

In this guide, I'll walk you through every native method for how to erase background on iPhone photos, step by step. No third-party apps. No subscriptions. Just the tools already sitting on your home screen.

Why Use Apple's Built-In Background Removal?

Before we dive into the how, let's talk about the why — because there are some real advantages to sticking with Apple's native tools:

• It's completely free. No watermarks, no "upgrade to Pro" nag screens.

• It's private. Your photos never leave your device or get uploaded to some random server.

• It's fast. Apple's on-device machine learning does the heavy lifting in about a second.

• It's integrated. Cutouts flow directly into Messages, Notes, Pages, Keynote, and more.

• No storage bloat. You're not installing another 200MB app you'll use twice.

The trade-off? Apple's tools give you less fine-grained control than dedicated editors. You can't manually refine edges or adjust feathering. But for most everyday use cases — social media posts, quick mockups, profile pictures — it's more than enough.

Method 1: The Visual Look Up Cutout (The Easiest Way)

This is the feature that made everyone say "wait, my iPhone can do that?" when it launched with iOS 16. Apple calls it the "subject lift" or "cutout" feature, and it's built directly into the Photos app.

Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Open the Photos app** and find the image you want to edit.

2. **Tap the photo** to view it full-screen.

3. **Press and hold on the subject** — the person, pet, object, or whatever you want to keep. You don't need to long-press any specific button; just touch and hold directly on the subject itself.

4. **Watch for the glow.** After about a second, you'll see a bright animated outline appear around the subject. That's iOS telling you it has identified the subject and separated it from the background.

5. **Lift your finger.** A context menu will pop up with options like Copy, Share, Add Sticker, and Save Image.

6. **Tap "Copy"** if you want to paste it elsewhere, or **"Share"** to send it directly to another app.

That's it. Seriously. You've just created a transparent-background cutout in under five seconds.

What Can You Do With the Cutout?

This is where things get fun. Once you've copied that cutout, you can:

• **Paste it into Messages** as an inline sticker.

• **Drop it into Notes** for quick collages or visual reminders.

• **Add it to a Pages or Keynote document** for presentations.

• **Save it as a sticker** by tapping "Add Sticker" — it'll live in your sticker drawer forever.

• **Paste it into another photo editor** (even a free one) to composite it onto a new background.

Tips for Best Results

The cutout feature is smart, but it's not magic. Here's how to get cleaner edges:

• **Use photos with clear subject-background contrast.** A person in a bright shirt against a plain wall works beautifully. A person in a camouflage jacket standing in the woods? Not so much.

• **Avoid busy backgrounds** with lots of similar colors to your subject.

• **Make sure the subject is well-lit** and in focus.

• **Portrait mode photos** often work exceptionally well because the depth data helps iOS understand what's foreground and what's background.

Method 2: Using Markup to Erase Backgrounds

The Visual Look Up method is great for lifting subjects, but what if you want to actually *erase* part of a background while keeping the rest of the image intact? That's where Markup comes in.

Markup is Apple's built-in annotation tool, accessible from Photos, Mail, Notes, and Screenshots. It's not a true AI background remover, but it gives you manual eraser tools that can clean up edges or remove unwanted elements.

How to Access Markup From Photos

1. **Open the photo** in the Photos app.

2. **Tap "Edit"** in the top-right corner.

3. **Tap the Markup icon** — it looks like a pen tip (you may need to tap the "..." menu first on some iOS versions).

4. **Select the eraser tool** from the bottom toolbar.

Using the Eraser

The Markup eraser works like a digital whiteout tool. It doesn't remove pixels to transparency — it paints over them with white (or whatever color you choose). This is useful for:

• **Cleaning up edges** after a cutout.

• **Removing small distractions** like a stray object in the corner.

• **Whitening backgrounds** for product photos.

The Limitations (Be Honest With Yourself Here)

Markup is not a replacement for real background removal. It's a manual tool, which means:

• **It's tedious** for complex shapes.

• **It doesn't create transparency** — you get a solid color instead.

• **It's not precise** at the pixel level.

So use it as a touch-up tool, not your primary background remover. For clean, transparent cutouts, stick with Method 1.

Method 3: Background Removal in Other Apple Apps

One of the coolest things about iOS's background removal is that it's not confined to the Photos app. Apple baked the subject-lift feature into several system apps, which opens up some handy workflows.

In Messages

You can create stickers directly from photos without ever leaving a conversation:

1. Open a conversation in Messages.

2. Tap the **"+"** or **Apps** button next to the text field.

3. Select **Stickers**.

4. Tap the **sticker icon with a "+"** to create a new one.

5. Choose a photo, and iOS will automatically lift the subject.

6. Add effects if you want, then tap **Add**.

Now you've got a custom sticker ready to spam your friends with.

In Notes

Notes is surprisingly powerful for quick image editing:

1. Open or create a note.

2. Tap the **camera icon** and choose **Photo Library** (or take a new photo).

3. Once the image is inserted, **long-press on the subject**.

4. Tap **Copy**, then paste it elsewhere in the note.

This is great for building visual mood boards or quick reference sheets.

In Safari and Other Apps

The subject-lift feature also works in Safari when you long-press an image on a webpage (though results vary depending on the image). It's a handy trick when you find an image online and want to grab just the subject.

Method 4: Saving Your Cutout as a Transparent PNG

Here's a question I see all the time: "How do I save the cutout with a transparent background?"

The answer is a little counterintuitive. When you use the Copy function, iOS copies the cutout with transparency intact. But if you just tap "Save Image" from the context menu, it may save a version that isn't transparent depending on where you save it.

Here's the reliable workflow:

1. **Long-press the subject** in Photos to create the cutout.

2. **Tap "Copy."**

3. **Open a blank note** in the Notes app.

4. **Paste the cutout** into the note.

5. **Tap the image** in the note, then tap **Share**.

6. **Choose "Save Image"** — this saves a PNG with transparency preserved.

Alternatively, you can paste the cutout directly into an app like Keynote, then export the slide as an image. This gives you a clean transparent PNG every time.

Common Problems and How to Fix Them

Even with Apple's polished tools, things don't always go smoothly. Here are the issues I hear about most often:

"The long-press isn't working."

• Make sure you're on **iOS 16 or later**. This feature doesn't exist on older versions.

• Try pressing and holding for a full second before lifting your finger.

• Some photos simply don't have a clearly identifiable subject. Try a different image.

• Restart your iPhone if the feature seems stuck.

"The cutout has rough edges."

• This usually happens with low-contrast images. Try a photo where the subject stands out more.

• Portrait mode photos tend to produce cleaner cutouts because of the depth data.

• You can clean up edges afterward using Markup's eraser tool.

"I can't save the cutout as a transparent image."

• Use the Notes app workaround described above.

• Avoid saving to your camera roll directly from the context menu if transparency matters.

"The feature disappeared after an update."

• Check that Background Removal is enabled in **Settings > Photos**. In some iOS versions, there's a toggle for this.

• Make sure you're not in Low Power Mode, which can disable some ML features.

FAQ: Removing Backgrounds on iPhone

Can I remove a background from an iPhone photo without any app?

Yes. Since iOS 16, the Photos app has a built-in subject-lift feature. Just long-press the subject in any photo, and iOS will separate it from the background. No third-party app required.

Does the iPhone background removal work on iOS 17, iOS 18, and iOS 26?

Yes, the subject-lift feature is available on iOS 16 and later, so it works on iOS 17, iOS 18, and iOS 26. The core functionality hasn't changed much, though newer versions have improved edge detection and sticker options.

How do I save a cutout with a transparent background?

Long-press the subject to lift it, then tap "Copy." Open the Files app, create a new file, and paste it — it will be saved as a PNG with a transparent background.

Why is the edge cutout not perfect around hair or fuzzy objects?

Apple's on-device ML model is great for clear edges, but fine details like individual hairs or fluffy fur can be challenging. If you need more precision, use our AI background remover tool for better edge quality.

Is the iPhone background removal private?

Absolutely. All processing happens on your device. Your photos never get uploaded to a server or shared with anyone.
