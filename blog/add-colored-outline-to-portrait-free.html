Common Mistakes To Avoid

Most people who try to add a colored outline to their portrait for the first time make the same few mistakes:

  • Forgetting to cut out the background first: If you run the outline tool on a regular photo with a busy background, the tool will trace the entire rectangular edge of the photo, not your face. Always run a background remover first to get a clean cutout of just your head and shoulders, then apply the outline — otherwise you'll get a weird colored border around the whole photo, not around your portrait.
  • Choosing an outline color that blends in: If you have dark brown or black hair, a black outline will disappear into your hair and look broken. Pick a contrasting color: white, bright blue, or your brand accent color. The whole point of the outline is to create contrast, so if it blends into your portrait, it defeats the purpose.
  • Making the outline too thick: Outlines wider than 25px start covering your hair and shoulders, and the sticker looks like a blob. Stick to 10-20px for profile pictures, and only go thicker if you're intentionally making a chunky cartoon sticker for TikTok or merch.
  • Exporting as JPG instead of PNG: JPG doesn't support transparent backgrounds, so your sticker will have a solid white square behind it, which ruins the floating sticker effect. Always export as a transparent PNG — that way the outline sits directly on top of whatever background you put it on.
