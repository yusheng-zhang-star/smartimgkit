How to Choose the Right Shadow Settings

For minimal modern UI design, use a low blur (5-10px), small offset (3-5px), and 20-30% opacity shadow. This creates a subtle lift without distracting from the element itself. For e-commerce product photos, use a larger blur (15-25px) and 40-50% opacity to create a natural grounding shadow that makes products look like they are sitting on a surface. For sticker designs, use a slightly larger offset and darker shadow color to mimic real sticker thickness. Avoid overdoing shadows: a heavy 50px blur with 80% opacity will look dated and tacky, even on product mockups.

Tips for Better Shadow Results

Always start with a clean, high-resolution transparent PNG before adding a shadow. If your cutout has jagged edges or leftover background pixels, the shadow will amplify those imperfections. For consistent branding across your website, save your favorite shadow preset values: for example, 8px vertical offset, 12px blur, 35% opacity black shadow works well for most UI elements. When designing for dark mode, use a slightly softer shadow with lower opacity, because shadows on dark backgrounds need less contrast to be visible. If you are creating multiple product images for an e-commerce store, use identical shadow settings across all product photos to create a cohesive, professional catalog look.
