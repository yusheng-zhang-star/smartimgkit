Why Does This Matter?

HEIC files are about half the size of equivalent JPGs, which is great for iPhone storage. But when you share photos with Windows users, upload to websites, or edit in older software, HEIC becomes a problem. Converting to JPG ensures compatibility everywhere. SmartImgKit converter preserves EXIF data (GPS, date, camera settings) during conversion.

HEIC vs JPG: What is the Difference?

HEIC (High Efficiency Image Container) is based on the HEVC video codec standard. It uses advanced compression techniques including intra-frame prediction, transform coding, and entropy encoding to achieve about 50% smaller file sizes than JPG at equivalent quality. This is why a 12MP iPhone photo in HEIC is typically 1.5-2MB, while the same photo as JPG is 3-4MB.

However, HEIC is not universally supported. Windows requires the HEIF Image Extensions app (a paid download from the Microsoft Store). Many older apps, websites, and printers do not recognize HEIC at all. JPG, on the other hand, is supported by virtually every device and software made in the last 25 years.

Why you shouldn't install sketchy HEIC converter apps

When Windows users first run into HEIC files, the first thing they often do is search for a free HEIC converter app to install. That's usually a mistake. Most of those free desktop converters come bundled with adware, browser hijackers, and even malware that asks for full photo library access "to convert your files." Some of them quietly upload your entire photo folder to their servers in the background. When you use a browser-based converter, there's nothing to install — the conversion runs locally in your browser using WebAssembly, and your photos never touch a remote server. No system permissions to grant, no updates to install, no background processes running on your computer. It's faster, safer, and doesn't leave junk files on your machine when you're done.

There's also a long-term storage angle to consider. If you're backing up years of iPhone photos to an external hard drive for long-term archival, converting everything to JPG before storing it means those photos will open on literally any device you'll own for the next 30 years. HEIC is still a relatively new format, and there's a real chance that in 20 years, no current software will support it — but JPG has been around for 30 years and will almost certainly be readable forever. For family photos, travel shots, and important memories, that compatibility is worth the small amount of extra storage JPG uses. You don't have to convert every photo on your phone, but for the ones you're archiving long-term, JPG is the safe choice. No signup, no watermarks, no file size caps, and your photos never leave your device during conversion.
