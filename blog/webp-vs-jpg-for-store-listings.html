WebP vs JPG for Store Listings (2026 Comparison)

Published: August 27, 2026 · 6 min read

WebP wins on your own Shopify or WooCommerce store — smaller files, faster pages, and roughly 97% browser support — while JPG stays the safer choice on marketplaces that don't accept WebP uploads. Pick wrong and you either waste load time or get a listing rejected at submission. This guide maps which platform wants which format and how to convert in bulk.

If you're running an ecommerce store, you've probably wondered: should I upload JPG or WebP to my listings? The answer isn't "one is always better" — it depends on which platform you're selling on. Get this wrong and you either waste load time on your own site, or get rejected when you try to upload WebP to Amazon. Here's the breakdown for every major platform in 2026.

WebP is technically better: smaller files, same quality, better modern compression. But not every platform accepts it. That's the catch.

Platform-by-Platform Rules

  • Amazon: Does NOT accept WebP. Only JPEG, PNG, TIFF, GIF. If you try to upload WebP, it will reject the file. Always use JPG at 2000×2000px for Amazon listings.
  • Shopify: Fully supports WebP. Uploading WebP to your own store makes pages load 30% faster, which improves your Google ranking and mobile conversion. Use our JPG to WebP converter to convert all your product photos before uploading to Shopify.
  • Etsy: Does NOT accept WebP. Only JPG, PNG, GIF. Use JPG at 2700×2000px for Etsy listings.
  • WooCommerce / self-hosted stores: Use WebP. Every browser made after 2020 supports it, and the smaller file sizes give you a clear speed advantage.
  • eBay: Does NOT accept WebP. Use JPG.
  • Instagram / social media: The platforms re-compress your uploads anyway, so JPG is fine. No need to convert.
The rule of thumb: if you're uploading to a third-party marketplace (Amazon, Etsy, eBay), use JPG. If you're uploading to your own website (Shopify, WooCommerce), use WebP.

Which Should You Choose for Your Own Store?

If you're running your own Shopify or WooCommerce store, WebP is the clear winner:

  • Smaller files mean faster page load times, which improves your Google Core Web Vitals score and boosts rankings.
  • 97% of your visitors are on browsers that support WebP. The 3% on old browsers (mostly old Android phones) will automatically fall back to JPG if you set up a picture element, but even without that, 97% is more than enough.
  • You don't lose any visual quality. WebP at 80% quality looks identical to JPG at 75% quality, but the file is 30% smaller.

Common Mistakes

  • Trying to upload WebP to Amazon/Etsy: It will get rejected. Always keep a JPG version of every product photo for marketplace uploads, and a WebP version for your own store.
  • Converting PNGs to WebP for transparency: WebP supports transparency, which is great — it's 50% smaller than transparent PNG for product cutouts. That's one of the best uses of WebP for ecommerce.
  • Over-converting: Don't convert photos that are already under 200KB. The file size savings are negligible, and you just add an extra step in your workflow.
  • Ignoring browser fallbacks: If you're coding your own site, add a <picture> tag with WebP as the first source and JPG as fallback. Shopify does this automatically, so you don't have to worry about it.

FAQ

Can I upload WebP images to Amazon? No. Amazon seller central only accepts JPEG, PNG, TIFF, and GIF. You have to upload JPG for Amazon listings. WebP only works on your own store and other platforms that support it.

Is WebP better than JPG for Shopify? Yes. Shopify fully supports WebP, and WebP files are 25-35% smaller than equivalent JPGs at the same visual quality. That means faster load times and better Core Web Vitals.

Does Etsy accept WebP images? No. Etsy only accepts JPG, PNG, and GIF. Use JPG for Etsy listings. If you use WebP on your own website blog, that's fine.
