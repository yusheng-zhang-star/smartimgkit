When to Use AVIF (and When Not To)

  • Use AVIF on your own store: Shopify, WooCommerce, and your own blog all support AVIF. Smaller files = faster pages = better Google ranking.
  • Don't use AVIF on Amazon/Etsy/eBay: These marketplaces don't accept AVIF. Use JPG for marketplace listings, and AVIF for your own store.
  • AVIF is perfect for hero images: Your main hero banner is the heaviest image on your page. Converting it to AVIF cuts its size by 50%, which gives you the biggest speed win.
  • Keep both versions: Keep a JPG version of every photo for marketplace uploads, and an AVIF version for your own site.

Common Mistakes

  • Converting every photo to AVIF: Photos under 100KB don't need conversion. Focus on your hero images and main product photos — those are the big files that slow down your page.
  • Setting AVIF quality too low: Below 70% quality, skin tones and product colors start to look banded. Stick to 75-85%.
  • Forgetting to test on mobile: After uploading AVIF to your store, test on an actual Android and iPhone to make sure the images load correctly. Most modern devices support it, but it's worth checking.
  • Uploading HEIC directly to your store: Most e-commerce platforms don't support HEIC. Always convert HEIC to a web-friendly format (AVIF/WebP/JPG) before uploading.

Why your iPhone saves photos as HEIC in the first place

If you've ever tried to AirDrop a photo to a Windows PC or upload it to an e-commerce platform, you've run into HEIC. Apple switched to HEIC as the default photo format in 2017, and for good reason: HEIC files are about half the size of equivalent JPGs, without any visible quality loss. That means you can fit twice as many photos on your 128GB iPhone, and each backup to iCloud takes half as long. It's a great format for storing photos on your phone. It's a terrible format for the web, because no major marketplace or older browser supports it. That's why conversion is a mandatory step for anyone who shoots product photos on their iPhone and sells online.
