Why Remove Objects from Photos?

There are countless reasons you might want to remove an object from a photo: a stranger photobombed your vacation picture, a power line ruins a beautiful landscape, a watermark covers part of an image, a price tag is visible on a product photo, or a blemish mars a portrait. Whatever the reason, the goal is the same — make the unwanted object disappear and fill the space with a background that looks like it was never touched.

Traditionally, this required expensive software like Adobe Photoshop and advanced skills with the clone stamp and healing brush tools. Pro Tips for Clean Removal

🎯

Overlap the mask

Paint 5-10% beyond the object's edge onto the background. This gives the model more context and prevents halo artifacts or visible seams around the removed area.

🔄

Multiple small passes

For complex scenes, remove objects one at a time rather than masking everything at once. Each pass gives the model a cleaner context to work with and produces better overall results.

🔍

Zoom in for detail

Use the zoom controls for small objects like text, blemishes, or thin wires. A precise mask on small elements produces cleaner edges and reduces the chance of affecting surrounding detail.

💡

Match lighting context

When removing an object in mixed lighting, include some of both lit and shadowed areas in the mask context. This helps the model generate a fill that matches the lighting direction of the surrounding scene.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Masking too precisely. Tracing the exact edge of an object leaves no context for the model. Always overlap slightly onto the background for cleaner results.
  • Removing everything in one pass. Complex scenes with multiple objects should be handled one object at a time. Removing everything at once can confuse the model and produce artifacts.
  • Using low-resolution source images. The model needs detail to generate realistic textures. Start with the highest resolution image you have for best results.
  • Expecting perfect results on faces. LaMa is excellent for objects and backgrounds, but for removing people from group photos where faces are adjacent, expect to do some manual touch-up or multiple passes.
  • Ignoring patterned backgrounds. Complex patterns like tiled floors, brick walls, or repeating textures may need 2-3 passes. Remove a section, let it fill, then remove the next section for the cleanest result.

Does the tool upload my photos to a server?

No. All processing happens locally in your browser using WebAssembly and WebGL. Your photo never leaves your device. This is the most privacy-friendly way to remove objects from photos, especially for sensitive or personal images.

Can I remove people from group photos?

Yes. Paint over the person you want to remove and the LaMa model will fill the space with the background. For best results when removing people adjacent to other people, use a smaller brush and multiple passes to avoid affecting the remaining subjects.

What image formats are supported?

JPG, PNG, and WebP are supported, up to 20MB. The output is always a full-resolution PNG to preserve quality. For very large images (above 4000px), processing may take 10-15 seconds.

How is this different from Photoshop Content-Aware Fill?

SmartImgKit uses the LaMa (Large Mask Inpainting) deep learning model, which is specifically trained for large-region inpainting. Photoshop Content-Aware Fill uses a different algorithm. Both produce good results, but SmartImgKit is free, requires no installation, and runs in the browser.
