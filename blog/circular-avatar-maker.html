Why every platform uses circular avatars anyway

Almost every modern social and work platform standardized on circular profile pictures starting around 2018, for two big reasons: first, they look consistent across different UI designs — a square avatar with sharp corners looks awkward next to rounded navigation buttons, chat bubbles, and card layouts. Second, circles feel more approachable and human: studies on profile photo perception consistently show circular headshots are rated as more friendly and trustworthy than square ones.

The problem: the platform's auto-crop is never as good as you can do yourself. By default, most platforms center the exact middle of your photo — which is usually your nose, not your eyes. On mobile, where most people view profiles, the avatar is even smaller, and the platform zooms in further. If you don't leave headroom and position your eyes correctly, you end up with a tiny sliver of your face floating in the circle, which looks terrible.

Common mistakes that make your avatar look bad

  • Centering your nose instead of your eyes. When you crop, put your eyes in the horizontal middle of the circle, not your nose. Our brain expects faces to have eyes near the top third of the frame, and centering the nose looks off.
  • Using a portrait or landscape photo as source. If your photo is 4:3 portrait, the circle will cut off your left and right ears. Start with a square photo, or leave enough margin around your face when you take it.
  • Exporting as JPG. As mentioned, JPG fills the transparent corners with white. On dark mode interfaces, that white square pops out and looks unprofessional. Always choose PNG.
  • Zooming too tight. If your face fills the entire circle, the platform's mobile zoom will cut off your forehead and chin. Leave 10-15% margin around your head.
  • Using a busy background. Your favorite concert photo looks great on your wall, but as a tiny 40px chat avatar, all you see is a jumble of lights. A simple, solid or softly blurred background makes your face pop.
