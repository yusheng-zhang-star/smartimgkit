The Problem With Old-School Upscaling

For years, most online image upscalers used bicubic interpolation. It sounds fancy, but what it actually does is simple: it takes existing pixels and averages them to guess the color of new pixels. The result? A bigger image that looks softer, not sharper.

❌ Bicubic Resizing
  • Just stretches existing pixels
  • Edges become blurry
  • Text gets fuzzy
  • No new detail created
✅ AI Super-Resolution
  • Reconstructs missing detail
  • Edges stay sharp
  • Text remains readable
  • Learns from millions of images

How Real-ESRGAN Changes Everything

Real-ESRGAN is a deep learning model trained on millions of image pairs — low-resolution originals and their high-resolution counterparts. Over time, it learns patterns: how skin texture looks at different scales, how text edges stay crisp, how natural gradients should flow.

AI upscaling does not stretch pixels — it reconstructs what a higher-resolution version would look like, based on patterns learned from millions of images.

How to Get the Best Results

  1. Start with the best source you have. A slightly blurry original produces far better results than a tiny thumbnail.
  2. Use 2x for most cases. It is fast and produces good results. Only go 4x when you need the resolution.
  3. Try the sharpen slider. 10-20% post-sharpening makes edges pop without artifacts.
  4. Do not upscale JPEG artifacts. If your source is heavily compressed, clean up first, then upscale.

When AI Upscaling Falls Short

AI upscaling is powerful, but it is not magic. It works by guessing detail — and sometimes it guesses wrong. Text in very small images can become garbled, fine patterns can get smoothed, and inputs under 200px leave too little information.

For these cases, try our AI Object Remover to clean distractions first, or Background Remover to isolate the subject before upscaling.
