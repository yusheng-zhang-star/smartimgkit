AI Image Upscaling with Real-ESRGAN: Make Blurry Photos Sharp Again

We just upgraded our image upscaler from basic interpolation to a real AI super-resolution model. Here is what changed, why it matters, and how to get the best results.

The Problem With Old-School Upscaling

For years, most online image upscalers used something called bicubic interpolation. It sounds fancy, but what it actually does is simple: it takes existing pixels and averages them to guess the color of new pixels. The result? A bigger image that looks softer, not sharper. You have probably seen this — enlarge a small photo and it turns into a blurry mess.

That is because interpolation cannot create detail that was not there. It can only spread out what already exists. For real sharpness, you need something that understands what the image is supposed to look like.

How Real-ESRGAN Changes Everything

Real-ESRGAN is a deep learning model trained on millions of image pairs — low-resolution originals and their high-resolution counterparts. Over time, it learns patterns: how skin texture looks at different scales, how text edges stay crisp, how natural gradients should flow.

When you feed it a blurry 800×600 photo and ask for 2x, it does not just stretch it. It reconstructs what a 1600×1200 version would look like, adding plausible detail based on everything it has learned. The difference is immediately visible — edges get sharper, textures reappear, and the image looks like it was originally shot at high resolution.

How to Get the Best Results

  1. Start with the best source you have. AI upscaling cannot fix a completely destroyed image. A slightly blurry original will produce far better results than a tiny thumbnail.
  2. Use 2x for most cases. It is fast and produces good results. Only go 4x when you genuinely need the resolution.
  3. Try the sharpen slider. A small amount of post-sharpening (10-20%) can make edges pop without introducing artifacts.
  4. Do not upscale JPEG artifacts. If your source is heavily compressed, upscaling will make the compression blocks more visible. Clean up first, then upscale.

When AI Upscaling Falls Short

AI upscaling is powerful, but it is not magic. It works by guessing detail — and sometimes it guesses wrong. You might notice:

  • Text in very small images can become garbled because the AI misreads letter shapes
  • Fine patterns (like fabric weaves) can get smoothed into a generic texture
  • Extremely low-res inputs (under 200px) leave too little information for the model
