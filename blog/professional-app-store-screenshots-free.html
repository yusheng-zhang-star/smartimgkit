The bad news: most designers charge $150–$300 per set. The good news: you can create conversion-optimized, Apple-approved App Store and Google Play screenshots for free, in under 5 minutes, without touching Figma or hiring anyone.

Your screenshots are the single most important asset on your app listing. The average user spends only 3 seconds scanning your store page before deciding whether to tap download or scroll away. Why raw phone screenshots never convert

Most first-time app developers make the same mistake: they open their simulator, press screenshot, and upload the raw PNG directly to App Store Connect. This fails for three reasons:

  • No context for new users. Someone who has never seen your app doesn’t know what they’re looking at. A bare screen of your settings page tells them nothing about your core value. You need 1–2 lines of short text explaining what they’re seeing, why it matters.
  • No device framing. Raw screenshots floating on a white background look unfinished. A clean device frame matching your UI makes the app feel polished, premium, and trustworthy within a fraction of a second.
  • Wrong dimensions. Apple updates required screenshot sizes every year with new iPone and iPad models. If you uploaded screenshots for iPhone 14, your 2026 listing will be stretched, cropped, or rejected when Apple rolls out new size requirements.

✅ What a good screenshot set includes

  • All required 2026 sizes: 6.9" iPhone (16 Pro Max), 6.5", 5.5", 12.9" iPad Pro, 13" iPad Air
  • Consistent brand colors and typography across all 3–5 screenshots
  • 1–2 short feature captions per frame, no walls of text
  • Clean device frames matching your UI design
  • Optional dark mode variants for apps that support it
  • Google Play required sizes exported in the same batch

⚠️ Common mistakes that kill conversion

  • Cramming 5+ lines of text on a single screenshot — most people won’t read it
  • Using blurry simulator screenshots scaled up to fit large device frames
  • Mismatched brand colors between the App Store listing and your app
  • Forgetting that 60% of users view the App Store on their phone, not desktop — text too small to read on a 5.5" screen means they scroll past
  • Repeating the exact same screenshot layout for every frame

Step-by-step workflow (5 minutes total)

  1. Take clean screenshots in your simulator first. Remove notifications, clear your status bar, and capture screens that show your core features in action — not your settings page, not your login screen. The first screenshot should always show your most popular, most impressive feature.
  2. Upload all your raw screenshots to the tool. Everything runs locally in your browser — your app screenshots never get uploaded to a server, no privacy risk.
  3. Pick a template that matches your brand. Choose from pre-built layouts for productivity, gaming, health, finance, and social apps. The tool automatically matches your brand colors, device frame style, and typography.
  4. Add 1–2 lines of short text per screenshot. Keep it punchy: "Track habits in 2 taps", "No account required", "Works offline". If it takes more than 8 words, cut it.
  5. Preview all device sizes. The tool automatically resizes and crops each screenshot for every required iPhone and iPad dimension, so text isn’t cut off or stretched.
  6. Download the full zip pack. Upload the whole folder to App Store Connect — it will accept every file on the first try, no size mismatch rejections.

Small tweaks that boost conversion 20%+

  • Lead with your strongest feature first. The first screenshot users see is the one that matters. Don’t open with a "Welcome!" screen — show your app’s core value in the first frame.
  • Match your app UI exactly. If your app uses dark mode, make the screenshot background dark. If your brand color is bright green, use that for your captions. Consistency builds trust.
  • Don’t overcrowd. 3–5 screenshots is the sweet spot. Any more and users won’t swipe all the way through. Any fewer and you don’t have enough space to show your features.
  • Show real UI, not fake mockups. Generic stock app screenshots look fake. Use your actual screens — imperfections make the app feel real and usable.
