Every photo taken with a phone or camera stores hidden EXIF metadata: GPS coordinates of where the photo was taken, your phone's unique serial number, exact time, camera settings. When you share a photo to social media or a marketplace, this metadata can leak your home address, location, and private information.

What EXIF Data is Hidden in Your Photos?

☑️ GPS location coordinates (exact address where you took the photo)
☑️ Device model, serial number, and operating system
☑️ Exact date and time the photo was taken
☑️ Camera settings (ISO, shutter speed, focal length)
☑️ Editing history if you used phone filters

Why You Should Strip EXIF Before Sharing

For sellers: sharing product photos with EXIF means competitors can see where and when you took the photos, and trace your studio location. For regular users: posting a photo of your house or kids with EXIF on Reddit means anyone can extract your exact home address.

FAQ

Does stripping EXIF reduce photo quality?

No. EXIF data is separate hidden metadata, removing it does not change a single pixel of the actual photo. The image looks identical, just without hidden information.

Do social media sites automatically strip EXIF?

Major platforms like Instagram and Facebook do strip EXIF on upload, but smaller marketplaces (Etsy, Mercado Libre, Vinted) do not — your location data stays attached.

Can I remove EXIF from phone photos on mobile browsers?

Yes. Our tool works on Android and iOS mobile browsers, all processing happens on your phone, no app download required.
