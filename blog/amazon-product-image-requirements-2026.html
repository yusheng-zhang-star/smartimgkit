Amazon Product Image Requirements 2026: What Every Seller Must Know

October 18, 2026 · 8 min read

Have you ever had a listing suddenly suppressed because your images didn't meet Amazon's rules? It happens more often than you think — Amazon's automated system scans every main image within hours of upload, and if it fails even one rule, your listing goes invisible until you fix it. Below is the complete 2026 checklist to avoid that.

The rules that get listings suppressed

  • Main image background must be pure white (RGB 255,255,255). No props, no scene backgrounds, no colored surfaces.
  • Minimum 1000×1000 pixels to enable the zoom feature. Amazon recommends 2000×2000px for sharpest results on mobile.
  • Product must fill 85% of the frame. No excessive white space around the product.
  • No text, logos, or watermarks on the main image — not even tiny "free shipping" badges.
  • No borders on the main image, no rounded corners.
  • JPEG format preferred (PNG and GIF also accepted, but JPEG loads faster for shoppers).

Most suppressed listings happen for three silly reasons: sellers add a promotional sticker to the main image, leave their product on a gray tabletop instead of white, or make the product tiny in the corner of a 2000px square. All three are caught automatically within 24 hours.

Why the zoom feature matters so much for conversion

One of the most underrated rules on Amazon is the 1000px minimum. That number isn't arbitrary — it's the minimum resolution required to unlock the hover-to-zoom feature on desktop and pinch-to-zoom on mobile. Sellers who enable zoom on their main images see conversion rates jump by 15-20% on average, because shoppers can zoom in to check material quality, stitching, tiny details, and imperfections before they buy. Without zoom, shoppers have to take your product description at face value, which creates doubt. Most new sellers upload 800x800 images just to get the listing live, and then wonder why their click-through rate is low — they're missing out on the single highest-impact optimization you can make to a listing.

Even if you hit the 1000px minimum, we recommend going to 2000x2000px. On modern high-resolution phone screens, a 1000px image gets upscaled to fill the screen, which makes it look slightly soft. A native 2000px image stays sharp even on the latest iPhone and Android displays, which makes your product look higher quality than competitors who only uploaded 1000px files.

Common mistakes that get new sellers in trouble

We've seen hundreds of new sellers get their first listing suppressed because of tiny, avoidable mistakes:

  • Adding tiny text badges like "New 2026" or "Free Shipping" to the main image. Amazon's OCR scans catch these even if the text is 10px tall, and it's an instant suppress — you'll have to remove the badge, reupload, and wait 1-2 days for the listing to be reactivated.
  • Shooting products on gray or off-white surfaces. The main image background has to be pure #FFFFFF, RGB 255,255,255. A light gray tabletop looks fine to your eye, but Amazon's scanner flags it immediately. That's why running the product through an AI background remover and placing it on a pure white canvas is so much more reliable than trying to shoot on a white sheet in bad lighting.
  • Leaving the product too small in the frame. Amazon requires the product to fill at least 85% of the square. If you're selling a small accessory like a phone charger and you leave 50% white space around it, the scan flags it as non-compliant. The listing tool automatically centers and scales the product to fill the frame correctly, so you don't have to eyeball it.
  • Using borders or rounded corners. Even a 1px light gray border around the main image will get suppressed. The image has to go edge to edge with pure white, no exceptions.

What to put in your secondary images

The main image has strict rules, but the 6 secondary image slots are where you actually convince people to buy. Top sellers follow a consistent order: image 2 shows the product in real use, image 3 shows size comparison with a common everyday object, image 4 is a close-up detail shot of texture or quality, image 5 shows dimensions marked on the product itself, image 6 shows everything that comes in the box, and image 7 covers any warranty or key feature. When you answer every common shopper question in the images, you cut down on pre-purchase messages and reduce return rates by 20% or more, because people know exactly what they're getting before they click buy.

Main image vs secondary images

The main image (slot 1) follows the strict rules above. Secondary images (slots 2-7) are where you actually sell: show the product in use, include size comparison with everyday objects, close-up detail shots, dimensions marked on the product, and packaging contents. Top sellers use secondary images to answer every buyer question before they even type it.
