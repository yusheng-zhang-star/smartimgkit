Why "no upload" actually matters

When you use a cloud remover, your photo goes to a server, gets processed, and the result comes back. You're trusting that server to delete your image, not train on it, not leak it, and not hand it over to a subpoena. For a meme photo this is fine. For an ID photo, a medical record, a customer headshot, or a product shot you're about to launch — it's a real risk.

SmartImgKit downloads the ISNet model once (~30–150MB, cached by your browser), then runs it locally. Your pixels never touch the network. The only data that leaves your device is the model download itself — which is a static file, not your photo.

🔒 Try it: open your browser's DevTools → Network tab, upload a photo to SmartImgKit. You'll see zero image upload requests. The only calls are the cached model and jsDelivr.

The cost math

Remove.bg charges roughly $0.20–0.50 per high-resolution cutout on the pay-as-you-go tier, or ~$9/month for a subscription that caps at 40 credits. If you process 100 images a month, that's $20–50/month. Over a year, that's $240–600.

SmartImgKit costs you $0. The compute runs on your own CPU/GPU. The only "cost" is the ~30MB model download on first visit, which your browser caches. This is why browser-based tools can be free forever — there's no server bill to pass on.

What happens when remove.bg shuts down?

On December 1, 2026, remove.bg goes dark. If your workflow, your API integration, or your design pipeline depends on it, you have about 60 days to migrate. The smart move is to switch now to a tool that won't disappear — one whose code is open source and whose files you can even self-host if you want.
