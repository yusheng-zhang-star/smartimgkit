How Batch Compression Works

  1. Select all the images you want to compress (up to 100 at a time).
  2. Set compression quality to 75% — the sweet spot where you can't tell the difference from the original.
  3. Check "resize to max 2000px" if your images are larger than that — it automatically resizes them and compresses them even smaller.
  4. Click compress. All images process locally in your browser, no upload to any server.

Why Compress Images Before Uploading?

  • Faster page speed: Smaller images mean your product pages load faster. Google ranks faster pages higher, and customers are more likely to wait for a page that loads in 2 seconds vs 10 seconds.
  • Faster uploads: Uploading 5MB photos to Amazon/Etsy takes forever. At 1MB per photo, uploads are 5x faster. If you have 100 listings, that's hours saved.
  • Meet platform limits: Most platforms have a 10MB file size limit. Your camera's original JPGs are 8-15MB, which gets rejected. Compress first.
  • Better mobile experience: Mobile users on slow connections will leave if your page takes too long to load. Compressed images load faster on mobile data.

For product photos, 75% is perfect. You can't tell the difference from the original when you view it on a screen, but the file is 2/3 smaller. Don't go below 60% — that's when you start to see pixelation and color banding.

Common Mistakes

  • Compressing too much: If you set quality below 60%, your product photos will look pixelated when customers zoom in on Amazon/Etsy. Stick to 70-80%.
  • Not resizing before compressing: A 4000×4000 photo compressed to 2MB is still 4000×4000px, which is way bigger than you need. Resize to 2000×2000 first, then compress — that cuts file size by another 50%.
  • Uploading uncompressed photos to your website: If your Shopify/WooCommerce store has 5MB product photos, your page speed score will be terrible. Compress all images before uploading.
  • Using online compressors that upload your photos: Most free compressors upload your photos to their server. Our tool runs locally, so your photos never leave your computer.

Why local batch processing beats cloud compressors

Most online batch compressors work by letting you select files, uploading them all to their server, compressing them there, then letting you download the results as a ZIP. Uploading 100 files totaling 500MB to a cloud server takes minutes, even on fast fiber — and on coffee shop wifi or mobile hotspot, it can take 20+ minutes. Local compression reads the files straight from your own hard drive, runs the JPEG encoding on your own CPU, and writes the compressed output directly to your Downloads folder. Compression settings that won't ruin your photos

New sellers often panic when they compress a photo and see the file size drop by 70%, assuming the image must be garbage now. The truth is that JPEG compression works by throwing away visual detail the human eye can't easily perceive — subtle color gradients in smooth backgrounds, tiny texture variations that are invisible on a phone screen. At 75% quality, those imperceptible details are removed, and the file shrinks dramatically, but when you view the compressed image on a screen, you literally cannot tell it apart from the original. It's only when you zoom in to 400% magnification and look at pixel-level edges that you might notice a slight softening — and no customer ever zooms that far in on a product photo. Amazon's own zoom feature is designed to work perfectly with 75% quality compressed photos, so you're not giving up any functionality.

The biggest mistake new sellers make is compressing too little, not too much. They leave their photos at 90% quality, which only cuts file size by 30%, and their site still loads slow. Dropping to 75% cuts it by 65%, with zero visible difference. If you're uploading to social media (Instagram, Pinterest, TikTok), you can even drop to 60% — those platforms compress photos heavily anyway when you upload, so starting from a smaller file means their compression doesn't degrade the quality as much.
