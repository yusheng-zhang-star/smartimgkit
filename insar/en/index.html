From SAR velocity points to geotechnical hazard report — all in one workbench

You processed the interferograms. MintPy, LiCSBAS or HyP3 handed you velocity points, coherence layers and time series. The engineering work starts exactly there — and that is where most workflows fall back to spreadsheets. This workbench finishes the job: geometric correction of LOS velocities, differential-settlement hazard classification, validation against GNSS and levelling, uncertainty budgets, and a report skeleton your reviewer will recognize. Every calculation runs locally in your browser — your survey points, velocities and CSV files are never uploaded, stored or transmitted.

Start with the LOS Converter See the full workflow

1 · LOS → Vertical Converter

Stop reporting line-of-sight velocities as settlement. Geometry-correct conversion with per-point incidence, slope projection and propagated uncertainty. CSV batch import, live vector diagram.

2 · Site Suitability Pre-Check

Before processing: expected coherence, layover/shadow and atmospheric scoring with transparent weights, red flags, and a ready-to-paste HyP3 job package.

3 · Gradient & Hazard Classifier

Differential settlement is what damages assets. Neighbour-gradient screening against four documented criteria packages, KML/CSV/PNG export.

4 · GNSS Validation

Match InSAR points to GNSS or levelling observations, compute RMSE, bias and residual spread, flag anomalies, and copy the standardized comparison paragraph.

5 · Error Budget Calculator

Five InSAR error sources combined by root-sum-square, contribution shares as a pie chart, and a summary paragraph for the quality-assurance section.

6 · Time-series Calculator

Cumulative displacement curves with linear baselines: per-point rates, acceleration detection by half-span comparison, and unwrapping-step flags.

7 · Coordinate Converter

WGS84 ↔ UTM batch conversion with automatic zone detection, CSV and GeoJSON interchange, proj4js precision — the bridge to CAD and GIS.

8 · Report Generator

Assemble the whole workflow into a structured screening note: four delivery scenarios, Data Tray statistics injection, standardized limitations language.

Why a local workbench

Monitoring data carries contractual and sometimes regulatory weight. Cloud tools that upload your point coordinates create obligations you may not be able to make — data-processing agreements, hosting jurisdiction questions, retention policies. This workbench takes a harder line: the computation runs in your browser's JavaScript and the data dies with the tab. There is no account, no database and no upload path in the code. What you keep are the exports — CSV, KML, GeoJSON, Markdown — which are yours the moment they are written.

The second commitment is professional honesty. Screening criteria are documented, thresholds are labelled as defaults, red flags state their physical mechanisms, and every tool's limitations section says what the tool cannot do. InSAR is a remarkable screening instrument and a poor oracle; the workbench is built to keep that distinction visible in every deliverable.
