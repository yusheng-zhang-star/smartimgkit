Detailed User Guide

Step 1: Upload Your Image

Click the upload area or drag and drop an image. Supported formats: JPG, PNG, WebP, GIF, BMP. The image loads instantly in your browser — nothing is uploaded to any server.

Step 2: Apply a Preset Filter

Click any of the 15 preset filter buttons to apply it instantly. Filters are applied non-destructively — you can switch between them or reset at any time. Available presets: None, Grayscale, Sepia, Invert, Blur, Vintage, Cool, Warm, Vibrant, Polaroid, Emboss, Sharpen, Hue Shift, Saturate.

Step 3: Fine-Tune with Sliders

Each slider gives you precise control:

  • Brightness (0-300%): Darken or brighten your image
  • Contrast (0-300%): Make colors more or less distinct
  • Saturation (0-300%): Control color intensity (0% = grayscale)
  • Blur (0-20px): Soften details for a dreamy effect
  • Hue Rotate (0-360°): Shift all colors around the color wheel
  • Opacity (0-100%): Control overall transparency

Step 4: Download

Choose your output format (PNG for quality, JPG for smaller files, WebP for modern compression) and click Download. The filtered image saves directly to your device.

Best Use Cases

  • Social media posts — Apply Vintage or Vibrant filters for eye-catching Instagram/Facebook photos
  • Product photography — Use Vibrant or Polaroid filters for e-commerce listings
  • Portrait touch-ups — Slightly increase brightness and saturation for better skin tones
  • Black & white conversion — Use Grayscale preset + contrast adjustment
  • Artistic effects — CombineBlur + Saturate for dreamy, stylized looks

Pro Tips

  • Preset filters and slider adjustments are combine — apply a preset first, then fine-tune with sliders
  • For portraits, increase brightness +5-10% and saturation +10-20% for a natural glow
  • Use the Vintage filter as a base, then reduce saturation slightly for a film photography look
  • Blur + high saturation creates a dreamy, impressionist painting effect
  • Reset any slider by double-clicking the value number next to the label
  • Save as PNG for maximum quality; use JPG at 90%+ quality for smaller files
