Detailed User Guide

Step 1: Upload Your Image

Load a JPG, PNG or WebP onto the canvas. It appears at full resolution and the brush is ready to use.

Step 2: Brush Over the Area

Click and drag across the region you want to hide. Two sliders control the effect: brush size (how wide the stroke is) and pixel size (how coarse the mosaic blocks are).

Step 3: Undo or Download

Made a mistake? Click Undo to step back. When you are happy, download the result as a PNG.

How the Pixelation Works

Each brush stroke takes the pixels underneath, shrinks them down to a tiny block of the chosen pixel size, then scales them back up with smoothing turned off. This produces hard-edged square blocks — the classic mosaic effect — instead of a soft blur.

Troubleshooting and Limitations

The brush follows your mouse or finger, so a steady hand gives the cleanest edges; enlarge the brush size to cover a region in one sweep. Undo steps back through your last few strokes, but it is not unlimited — get the key areas right before exporting. Output is PNG, which preserves the crisp mosaic edges without JPEG smear.

Who Uses Pixelate Image

Journalists and moderators obscure bystanders before publishing, support staff hide personal data in screenshots, and everyday users blur licence plates or addresses in photos they share. The local brush keeps everything private from start to finish.

Choosing the Right Pixel Size

A small pixel size (for example 8) gives a fine, densely-blocked mosaic that is harder to see through but still clearly obscured; a large pixel size (32 or more) produces bold, chunky squares. Match the pixel size to the level of detail you are hiding — a licence plate needs only a coarse block, while a face may benefit from a finer setting to look intentional rather than glitchy.
