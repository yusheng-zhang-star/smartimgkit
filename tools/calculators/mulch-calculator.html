How Much Mulch Do You Actually Need?

Mulch keeps weeds down, holds moisture in soil, and makes your garden bed look clean. But most people buy way too much or too little. The rule of thumb for most garden beds is 2-3 inches of mulch: any deeper and you'll suffocate plant roots, any thinner and weeds will grow right through.

We calculate total cubic feet by multiplying bed width × bed length × mulch depth (converted to feet), then convert to cubic yards (divide by 27, since 1 yard = 27 cubic feet). We also calculate the number of standard 2 cubic feet bags sold at every hardware store, so you know exactly how many bags to load in your car.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Don't put mulch too deep. More than 4 inches of mulch will block air from reaching plant roots, and can cause root rot. 3 inches is perfect for most flower beds and vegetable gardens.

Don't forget to measure curves. If your bed is not a perfect rectangle, measure the longest and widest points, and add 10% extra for curved edges. Most people measure too tight and end up short by a bag or two.

Bulk is cheaper for big beds. If you need more than 3 cubic yards, buying bulk mulch delivered is almost always cheaper than buying individual bags. For small beds under 2 cubic yards, bags are easier to transport.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many bags of mulch for a 10x10 bed?

A 10x10 bed with 3 inches of mulch needs about 14 standard 2 cubic foot bags, or 1 cubic yard of bulk mulch.

Is 3 inches of mulch enough?

Yes, 3 inches is the ideal depth for most garden beds. It blocks weeds, holds moisture, and won't suffocate plant roots.

How much does 1 cubic yard of mulch cover?

1 cubic yard covers 100 square feet at 3 inches depth, or 324 square feet at 1 inch depth.
