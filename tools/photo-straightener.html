Detailed User Guide

Step 1: Upload Your Image

Click the drop zone or drag in a JPG, PNG or WebP. The image is drawn to the canvas at its original dimensions.

Step 2: Rotate to Straighten

The angle slider rotates the image around its centre by degrees, from negative (counter-clockwise) to positive (clockwise). The canvas clears and redraws on every change so you see the correction in real time.

Step 3: Download Your Result

Click Download to export a JPG. Note that rotating leaves transparent corners where the image no longer fills the frame — crop the result or add a border with the Image Border tool if that bothers you.

How Much Rotation Do You Need?

Most tilted horizons need only 0.5–3°. Rotate in small increments and use a straight edge in the scene — a shoreline, a wall, a table edge — as your visual reference for level.

How the Rotation Is Applied

The image is rotated around its centre using a canvas transform, so the midpoint of your photo stays fixed while the edges swing. This is the same geometric operation professional editors use, just exposed as a single degree slider. The canvas clears and redraws on every tick, which is why the preview always shows the current angle exactly.

Troubleshooting and Limitations

Because the tool rotates within the original frame, the corners where the image no longer covers the canvas are left empty rather than auto-cropped or auto-filled. If those empty corners bother you, follow up with the Cropper to trim them or the Image Border tool to fill them with a colour. Very large photos redraw more slowly, since the full-resolution canvas is recomputed on each slider change.

When Straightening Helps Most

Landscape and seascape shots with a visible horizon gain the most, since the eye reads a tilted horizon instantly. Architectural photos benefit when verticals lean slightly inward. Product shots benefit when a table edge or background line runs at a subtle angle and distracts from the item itself.
