Compress an image to 200 KB without sending it anywhere

This page does one job: get your file to 200 KB or less. The target-size field is already set to 200, so you only have to pick a file. The compression runs inside your browser — nothing is uploaded and no account is needed.

📋

Where the 200 KB limit comes from

CMS and blog platforms often warn above 200 KB per uploaded image because it affects page weight. Product and listing back offices commonly require images under 200–500 KB. Email clients and shared inboxes start to feel sluggish with inline images over a few hundred KB each.

⚠️

Why the file keeps going over 200 KB

Exporting from an editor at 4000 px and assuming 200 KB is achievable at that resolution. Staying on PNG for photographs, which easily multiplies the size by three or more. Compressing once at a fixed quality and never checking the actual output size.

🔒

Private by design

There is no upload step and no server round trip. The image is decoded, re-encoded and offered back to you as a download, all inside the page. That also means it keeps working when a locked-down portal or an inbox is the only thing you have access to.

Compressing to 200 KB in Detail

Note on mechanics: the tool re-encodes your file at up to eight JPEG/WebP quality levels between roughly 10% and 95% and keeps the smallest result that lands at or under 200 KB. It never changes pixel dimensions. At 200 KB the search normally settles comfortably above the lowest quality steps, so photographs keep clean gradients.

Why 200 KB is the comfortable middle

At 200 KB and a 1600 px long edge a photograph gets roughly 0.1 bytes per pixel, which is generous enough that most viewers cannot tell it apart from the original on screen. This is the point where compression stops being a visible compromise and becomes ordinary image optimisation. It is also why CMS platforms use it as a warning threshold rather than a hard block.

The relationship between size, dimensions and sharpness

Those three move together, and only two of them are under your control on this page. The tool adjusts quality, you adjust dimensions, and the resulting file size follows. If sharpness matters more than weight, hold the dimensions and accept a slightly larger file. If weight matters more, reduce dimensions and the same quality setting gets you a much smaller result.

Checklist for web and product images at 200 KB

  • Resize to the size the destination displays — usually 1200–1600 px.
  • JPG or WebP output; PNG only when transparency is genuinely required.
  • Crop to the intended aspect ratio before compressing, not after.
  • Note the saved percentage; if it is under 20% the image was already optimised.

Where the metadata goes

Re-encoding an image through a canvas — which is what this tool does — rebuilds the pixel data and writes a fresh, minimal container around it. Most embedded metadata, including camera make, timestamps and GPS coordinates, is not carried over as a side effect. If stripping metadata is the actual goal rather than a byproduct, use the dedicated EXIF Remover, which is explicit about it.

Why 200KB Is the Standard Image Size Limit

Most e-commerce platforms, website CMS systems, and email marketing tools cap uploads at 200KB per image to keep pages loading fast. Product photos that are 1-2MB slow down mobile page load times by multiple seconds, which directly hurts conversion rates on store pages. Our compressor fine-tunes JPEG/WebP quality level to hit exactly 200KB without visibly blurring the image — you don't have to manually tweak quality sliders and re-export over and over.

Because all compression runs locally in your browser, your product photos and personal images never get uploaded to our servers. There's no file upload queue, no account required, and no watermark on compressed images. You can process as many photos as you need for Amazon listings, Etsy shops, or website uploads, completely free.

For photographers sending proofs to clients, the tool also works for resizing and compressing batches of wedding or portrait photos to fit email attachment limits. There's no limit on how many images you can process each day, and the compressed files keep enough quality for web use without the visible artifacts you get from other online compressors.
