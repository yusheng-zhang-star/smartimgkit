Detailed User Guide

Step 1: Upload Your Image

Click the drop zone or drag in a JPG, PNG or WebP. The image loads onto the canvas and is inverted immediately.

Step 2: Review the Inversion

Each pixel's red, green and blue channels are subtracted from 255, flipping light to dark and hue to its complement. This is a full, exact inversion — not a stylised approximation.

Step 3: Download

Click Download to export the result as a PNG. Your original file is never modified.

When Inversion Looks Best

High-contrast images and graphics invert most dramatically. For photos, the effect can be intense; try it on already-bold subjects rather than low-contrast scenes.

How the Inversion Is Computed

For every pixel, the red, green and blue channels are each replaced with 255 minus their original value. This is a pure per-channel inversion with no gamma correction or artistic remapping, so the result is a mathematically exact negative — the same operation used to describe film negatives.

Troubleshooting and Limitations

Inversion is a global operation, so you cannot restrict it to part of the image within this tool; the whole photo flips at once. On photos with a narrow tonal range, the negative can look muddy because mid-tones map to other mid-tones — high-contrast sources produce the boldest results. Output is PNG, which preserves the exact inverted values without JPEG artifacts.

Who Uses Inversion

Photographers digitising film invert scans as a step toward a positive. Artists use the negative look for experimental, graphic posters and album art. Designers invert logos and line drawings to quickly produce a white-on-black variant or pull a selection mask.
