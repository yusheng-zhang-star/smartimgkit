How text removal actually works

Behind the "Remove Text" button is a reconstruction model called LaMa (Large Mask Inpainting), running entirely in your browser. Understanding the three steps below is what separates a clean removal from a smudged one.

1. You paint the mask

The brush marks the pixels you want gone — the letters plus a sliver of their edge. The mask is the only instruction the model gets: it does not "read" the text or recognize glyphs, it treats everything under the brush as a hole to fill.

2. The model crops to the region

Instead of processing the whole photo, the engine crops to the masked area's bounding box plus a 128-pixel margin, then runs inference on that region at 256–512 px. This region-of-interest pass is why a removal takes seconds rather than minutes.

3. LaMa reconstructs the background

The ONNX model, executed through WebAssembly and ONNX Runtime Web, synthesizes new pixels from the surrounding texture so the background continues across the hole. It invents a plausible continuation — it does not recover detail that was never in the image.

The model is downloaded once and cached by your browser, then runs offline. Images larger than 1280×720 are downscaled for the inpainting pass and restored to full resolution on export, and you can undo up to 20 brush strokes.

What it handles well — and what it can't

Any text remover is a reconstruction problem: the model has to invent the pixels the letters were covering. It is honest to say where that works and where it needs help.

✅ Comes out clean

Text on flat or gradient backgrounds: product photos, screenshots, slides, price stickers, single-line captions, subtitles on a still frame, small wordmarks.

🔁 Worth a second pass

Dense paragraphs over paper grain, text crossing fine detail such as hair, foliage or brickwork, and very large lettering that covers more than a third of the frame. Mask one block, run it, then repeat.

🎯 Tips that matter

Cover the letters plus one or two pixels of the edge — leaving a sliver of the glyph behind is the most common reason a removal looks imperfect. Remove light text and dark text in separate passes.
