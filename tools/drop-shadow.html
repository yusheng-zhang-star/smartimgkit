Detailed User Guide

Step 1: Upload a PNG

Use an image with a transparent background for the cleanest result. It loads onto the canvas and the shadow is applied immediately.

Step 2: Shape the Shadow

Three sliders control the effect: offset (how far the shadow sits from the subject), blur (how soft the edges are) and opacity (how dark it is). A small offset with high blur gives a natural soft shadow; a large offset with low blur reads as a hard, graphic drop.

Step 3: Download

Export the result as a transparent PNG so the shadow composites cleanly over any background you place it on.

How the Shadow Is Drawn

The tool draws the image, then paints a blurred, semi-transparent copy of its silhouette offset beneath it. Because the shadow is derived from the actual shape of the PNG, it follows irregular cut-outs correctly rather than being a fixed rectangle.

Troubleshooting and Limitations

The shadow follows the alpha (transparency) of your PNG, so images without transparency will shadow as a full rectangle. Offset, blur and opacity are global — you cannot bend or skew the shadow direction within this tool. Output is PNG to preserve the transparency and soft shadow edges.

Who Uses Drop Shadow

Product photographers add grounding shadows before listing items, designers lift logos off hero sections, and anyone making thumbnails uses a shadow to make a subject stand out against a busy background.

Matching Shadow to Light Source

A convincing shadow points away from your scene's light source. If your product is lit from the top-left, the shadow should fall toward the bottom-right and stay subtle; a shadow cast in the wrong direction makes the image look flat or wrong, even if viewers cannot say exactly why. Keep the opacity low (under 40%) for a natural look and reserve a hard, dark shadow for bold graphic designs.
