📥 What is a GPX file?

GPX (GPS Exchange Format) is a standard file format that stores GPS coordinates — in this case, the locations where your photos were taken.

🖥️ Open on computer

  • Google Earth Pro (free) → File → Open → select .gpx
  • GPS Visualizer → gpsvisualizer.com (upload & view online)
  • QGIS (professional GIS software)

📱 Open on phone

  • Google Earth app → Menu → Projects → Import
  • Organic Maps / Maps.me → Import GPX
  • Strava / Komoot → Upload as route

📝 What's inside?

Each photo becomes a <wpt> (waypoint) with its latitude, longitude, filename, and timestamp. You can open it in any text editor to see the raw data.

💡 Tip: Import the GPX into Google Earth to see all your photo locations pinned on a satellite map — great for reliving your trip!

Frequently Asked Questions

How does photo location map work? +
When you upload a photo, our tool reads the EXIF metadata embedded in the image file. Most smartphones and digital cameras automatically record GPS coordinates when a photo is taken. We extract these coordinates and display them as photo thumbnails on an interactive map powered by OpenFreeMap & OpenStreetMap.
Are my photos uploaded to a server? +
No! All processing happens directly in your browser. Your photos never leave your device. This means your location data stays completely private and secure.
Why don't some photos show GPS data? +
Photos may lack GPS data if location services were disabled on the camera/phone, if the photo was edited and metadata was stripped, or if the camera doesn't have GPS capability. You can check our EXIF viewer tool to see all metadata in a photo.
Can I share my photo map? +
Yes! Click the "Share Map" button to generate a shareable link. The link includes the GPS coordinates so recipients can see the same map view. Note: photos themselves are not shared, only the locations.
What makes this photo map different? +
Unlike simple map viewers, our tool displays your actual photo thumbnails as map markers. Click any marker to see a larger preview with full details. It's like seeing your travel photos pinned exactly where they were taken!
