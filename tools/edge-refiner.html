Detailed User Guide

Step 1: Upload a Cut-out

Load a transparent PNG. The tool is designed for cleaning up existing cut-outs, not for making the initial background removal.

Step 2: Paint Over the Edges

Zoom into the edge and paint over white halos, jagged pixels or leftover background. The brush replaces the painted area with transparency, restoring a clean edge.

Step 3: Undo or Download

Use Undo to step back through your strokes, then download the refined PNG when it looks right.

How the Refining Works

The tool gives you a pixel-level brush over the image. Painting erases the painted region (turning it transparent), so you can chip away white fringes and rough edges manually. A zoom control lets you work precisely at the edge.

Troubleshooting and Limitations

This is a manual, hand-cleaning tool — it will not automatically re-segment the subject. For large areas, use a larger brush; for fine detail, zoom in and shrink the brush. Undo steps back through your recent strokes but is not unlimited. Output is a transparent PNG.

Who Uses Edge Refiner

Designers clean up auto-removed backgrounds that left a halo, product photographers fix cut-out edges before listing items, and anyone compositing a subject onto a new background tidies the boundary first.

Working Efficiently on Long Edges

For long, straight edges, zoom out slightly and use a steady drag rather than many short clicks — a single continuous stroke leaves a smoother line. Switch between a large brush to clear broad halos and a small brush to chase individual pixels. Because painting erases to transparency, work from the outside of the subject inward so you chip away the fringe without cutting into the subject itself.
