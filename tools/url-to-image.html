Detailed User Guide

Step 1: Enter the URL

Type the full address, including https:// if you know it. If you omit the scheme, the tool adds https:// automatically.

Step 2: Wait for the Page to Load

The page loads inside a preview frame in your browser. Give it a few seconds to finish loading images and layout before capturing.

Step 3: Download the Image

Click the download button to render the frame's content to a canvas and save it as an image file.

How the Capture Works

The page is loaded in an embedded frame and converted to an image using a client-side HTML-to-canvas library. Rendering happens locally in your browser — no screenshot server is involved.

Troubleshooting and Limitations

Many sites block themselves from being embedded in a frame (a security policy called X-Frame-Options or Content-Security-Policy), and cross-origin content cannot always be read by the canvas. When a page refuses to load or capture, the result may be blank or partial — this is a limitation of capturing third-party sites from any browser, not a bug in this tool. For best results, capture pages you control or that permit embedding.

Who Uses URL to Image

Marketers capture landing pages for campaign reviews, developers attach screenshots to bug reports, and designers save reference snapshots of sites they like. It is a quick way to freeze a page's current state as a shareable image.

Capturing Your Own Pages

When you capture a page on your own site or a page that permits framing, the result is a clean, full-width render of what the page actually looks like. This is useful for creating a visual changelog, saving a campaign's landing page before it goes live, or building a quick gallery of your work without taking manual screenshots.
